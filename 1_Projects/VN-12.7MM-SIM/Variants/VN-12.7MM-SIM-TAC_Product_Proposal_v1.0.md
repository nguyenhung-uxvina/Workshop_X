# VN-12.7MM-SIM-TAC — PRODUCT PROPOSAL

## 12.7mm Tactical Crew Gunnery Trainer (BẠCH ĐẰNG)

---

## 1. PRODUCT SUMMARY

- **Product name:** 12.7mm Tactical Crew Gunnery Trainer
- **Codename:** BẠCH ĐẰNG (Battle of Bach Dang River — Vietnam's decisive naval victory)
- **Product code:** VN-12.7MM-SIM-TAC
- **Primary function:** Enable Vietnamese Navy 12.7mm patrol boat crews (gunner + loader + commander) to develop coordinated multi-threat engagement proficiency through networked simulation — without ammunition, ship availability, or fair-weather dependency.
- **Customer (who buys):** Vietnamese Navy Training Command — procured alongside or as upgrade from I-01 Standard units. 2–3 TAC units per training center (one per operating crew type).
- **End-user (who uses):** Full 3-person 12.7mm crew: **gunner** (fires), **loader** (belt feed, malfunction, ammo call), **boat commander** (threat priority, engagement authorization, ceasefire). Same 600-gunner pool as I-01 — plus loaders and commanders (~1,200 additional personnel). Total addressable training population: ~1,800 vs 600 for I-01.
- **Target market:** Vietnamese Navy training centers (same 6–8 facilities as I-01). Phase 2 hardware expansion — sold alongside I-01 installations.

## 4. WORKING PRINCIPLE OPTIONS (Phase 2 Selects)

### Option A: Wired 3-Station Fixed Configuration ← RECOMMENDED

- I-01 gunner station + commander desk console + loader bench station, all wired LAN (≤1ms latency, well within 50ms requirement)
- Fixed floor plan: training room configured for 3-station triangle layout matching patrol boat crew positions
- **Lowest technical risk** — wired eliminates wireless latency variance; deterministic sync
- **Highest fidelity** — physical commander console + physical loader bench (not just screen)
- **Cost delta over I-01:** ~$18–22K [ASSUMPTION]
- Rationale: Establishes crew coordination as designed-in feature, not afterthought. Wired baseline can be retrofitted with wireless in later variant without architectural change.

### Option B: Wireless Flexible Configuration

- Same 3 stations but commander + loader are tablet-based, WiFi-linked
- Flexible — reconfigure for different crew position drills; deployable in smaller rooms
- WiFi latency risk (~10–30ms) — within 50ms budget but with variance; requires latency testing
- **Cost delta over I-01:** ~$15–18K (tablets cheaper than fixed consoles, but SW networking harder)

### Option C: Hybrid Fidelity (Full Gunner + Simplified Software Crew)

- I-01 full-fidelity gunner station + commander/loader as PC interface only (no physical replica hardware)
- Fastest to build — crew positions are software-only
- Lowest crew immersion: commanders use keyboard/mouse, loaders click buttons. Fails ODI O07 for non-gunner positions.
- **Cost delta over I-01:** ~$10–14K | Faster delivery but weakest differentiation vs scripted software solutions

**Phase 2 recommendation: Option A.** Rationale: VN-12.7MM-SIM-TAC's competitive advantage is that it trains the physical crew role — not just cognitive awareness. Option C undermines this. Option B adds latency risk without decisive cost saving. Option A delivers the full value proposition at moderate cost delta.

## 5. RISK ASSESSMENT

### Technical: MEDIUM

- **Top concern:** Multi-station real-time sync at ≤50ms across 3 stations during dynamic scenario. Unity/Godot networking layer must be benchmarked early. If COTS networking lib cannot meet latency target reliably, requires custom socket implementation — scope expansion risk.
- **Secondary:** Crew scoring algorithm is novel — no reference design. Defining crew-level performance metrics (command latency, role adherence, communication timing, fire discipline) requires gunnery instructor + tactical doctrine input. Risk: metrics may not align with what Navy evaluators actually score.
- **Tertiary:** Loader station physical fidelity — DShKM belt-feed mechanism replica must correctly simulate ≥3 malfunction types with physical remediation. Mechanical design effort and user-acceptance testing not trivial.

### Schedule: MEDIUM

- **Top concern:** Blocked at Phase 2 until I-01 Phase 3 ACCEPTED (DG-1). If I-01 slips, TAC Phase 2 slips proportionally. I-01 is on 12-month timeline — TAC Phase 2 cannot start before month 6–9.
- **Mitigation:** TAC Phase 1 (crew doctrine research, requirements list, loader station spec, crew scoring algorithm design) can proceed in parallel with I-01 Phase 2–3 immediately. This front-loads intellectual work so TAC Phase 2 can start within 2–4 weeks of DG-1 clearing.

### Market: LOW

- **Top concern:** Navy procurement of TAC may wait for I-01 to be proven in service — 6–12 months after I-01 delivery before TAC is ordered. Budget planning should assume TAC revenue starts at month 24–30, not month 18.
- **Opportunity (no competitor risk):** No NATO product has crew coordination capability at any price tier. VN-12.7MM-SIM-TAC is a **price-independent differentiator** — if the Navy wants crew training, TAC is the only option globally at this price. No price competition exists.

### Kill Condition

> **Automatic kill:** If I-01 Standard Fixed Trainer fails sim-to-live validation (r² < 0.50) after full tuning cycle, TAC is automatically cancelled — TAC inherits I-01's SW core and ballistic model. No valid I-01 → no TAC.
>
> **Independent kill:** If crew coordination training pilot shows <10% improvement over individual-only training in head-to-head comparison after 2 full tuning iterations, the core value proposition (crew synergy training meaningfully improves crew performance) is not demonstrated. Pivot to I-01 individual expansion instead of crew variant.
