# IRONMESH — 30-Second General Briefing

> **Purpose:** Compress the trust-building cycle (B2) by increasing touchpoint frequency.
> Not one big presentation — many low-stakes, high-density interactions.
>
> **Test:** If you can deliver this in 30 seconds and the listener asks a follow-up question,
> the briefing worked. If they nod politely and look elsewhere, iterate.
>
> **The Feynman constraint:** If a Vietnamese military general cannot understand it, the
> problem is in the explanation, not the general. Re-simplify.

---

## CORE 30-SECOND BRIEFING (English — ~65 words, ~30s spoken)

> **Use for:** First introduction at any military, procurement, or technical meeting.

---

*"General, AI is like a very fast, very obedient staff officer who sometimes makes confidently wrong decisions.*

*Our system — IRONMESH — uses AI for the parts that need speed and consistency: counting bullet impacts, tracking targets, computing firing solutions.*

*But we always keep a Vietnamese officer in the decision loop for three things: safety judgment, tactical context, and qualification decisions.*

*The AI makes the training faster and cheaper. The officer makes it correct and accountable."*

---

**Word count:** 67 words | **Spoken pace:** 130 wpm = **31 seconds** ✓
**Listener test:** Do they ask "how much does it cost?" or "can we see a demo?" → briefing worked.

### Variant A — Formal Ceremony / First Introduction (current core version)

→ Use the core version above. Full 30 seconds.

### Variant B — Informal / Side Conversation (~15 seconds)

*"We build AI-powered training systems for the military — but the officer always has final say. The AI makes training cheaper and faster. The officer makes it accountable. That's the design principle."*

**When to use:** Side conversation at an event, introductions before a formal meeting, chance encounter. Get to the hook fast, leave them wanting more.

---

### Variant C — Procurement / Budget Meeting Opening (~20 seconds)

*"Our system does what a $5 million foreign training system does — but costs $25,000 and runs offline. The reason it's possible: we use AI for the repetitive work, and keep your officers in control of the decisions that matter. The military pays for the hardware once. The AI gets smarter with every session."*

**When to use:** When the context is cost comparison or budget. Lead with the price delta, explain WHY it's possible.

---

### Variant D — Technical Audience (~30 seconds)

*"IRONMESH is a multi-agent AI platform. LOMAH handles acoustic shot scoring, CAM handles vision analytics, CORTEX fuses the data streams. Every subsystem is automated. Every gate decision — qualification pass/fail, engagement authorization, safety flag — is human-in-the-loop by design. The architecture isn't a safety add-on. It's why the Vietnamese military will trust it."*

**When to use:** Defense engineers, procurement technical reviewers, MoD technical officers. Lead with architecture.

---

### Variant E — After a Live Demo (~15 seconds close)

*"What you just saw is Phase 1. The data the system just collected feeds the AI that predicts which soldiers need more training before they're combat-ready. That's Phase 5 — but it starts with the data from this session."*

**When to use:** After showing SCOREBOARD/PULSE live. Seed the vision of PROPHECY without overselling what's built yet.

## 60-SECOND EXPANSION (when they say "tell me more")

*"Right now, training a Vietnamese infantry unit for live-fire qualification takes 3 days, costs significant ammunition, and produces a paper report that nobody reads. Our system — IRONMESH — compresses that.*

*The acoustic sensor counts every bullet impact in real time. The camera AI shows the commander which soldier has a flinching problem and which has a sight alignment problem. The system generates the after-action report automatically — in Vietnamese — in 30 seconds.*

*We're not replacing the range officer. We're giving them a tool that makes every training session produce five times the data, at the same cost.*

*In Phase 2, the system starts predicting which soldiers will fail their qualification 30 days before the test. That means commanders can intervene early — with the right training — before the failure happens.*

*That's the difference between a training range and a readiness system."*

**Word count:** ~150 words | **Spoken pace:** 130 wpm = ~70 seconds
**Purpose:** Move from interest to desire. This version sells the PROPHECY vision.

## PRACTICE PROTOCOL

> **Target:** Deliver core briefing from memory, naturally, without rushing.
> **Schedule:** Monday / Wednesday / Friday — 10 minutes each session.
> **Total investment:** 30 min/week.

### Session Structure (10 minutes)

```
Minutes 0–2:  Cold delivery — no notes, no warmup. Record on phone.
Minutes 2–5:  Listen back. Score yourself on rubric below.
Minutes 5–7:  Identify ONE specific thing to improve (not everything — one thing).
Minutes 7–9:  Deliver again with that one change applied.
Minutes 9–10: Write 1 line in practice log: what changed, what still needs work.
```

### Self-Assessment Rubric

| Criterion | 1 — Not yet | 3 — Developing | 5 — Ready |
|-----------|------------|----------------|-----------|
| **Memory** | Needed notes or stopped | Slight hesitation, recovered | No notes, no gaps |
| **Pace** | Rushed (<25s) or slow (>40s) | 28-35 seconds, slightly uneven | 28-32s, natural rhythm |
| **Analogy** | "staff officer" analogy skipped | Included but mechanical | Delivered as if it's your own idea |
| **HITL line** | Skipped "officer in decision loop" | Included but rushed | Delivered with emphasis — this is the trust-building moment |
| **Closing** | Trailed off | Delivered but flat | "The officer makes it correct and accountable" — lands with conviction |
| **Eye contact** | Looking down or away | Intermittent | Direct contact on HITL line and close |

**Score 5/5 on Memory + HITL line = minimum bar for a real meeting.**
**Score 5/5 on all = ready for a general.**

### Common Failure Modes

| Failure | Symptom | Fix |
|---------|---------|-----|
| **Rushing** | Delivered in <22 seconds | Pause after "wrong decisions." Full stop. Breathe. Then continue. |
| **Jargon creep** | "multi-agent inference pipeline" sneaks in | Replace with "counting bullet impacts" — always concrete, always physical |
| **Skipping the HITL line** | Went straight from AI description to result | The HITL line IS the trust signal. Never skip. It's the reason a Vietnamese general will say yes. |
| **Flat close** | "...the officer makes it correct and accountable" delivered without weight | This is the ACH philosophy in one sentence. Deliver it like you mean it. Pause before it. |
| **Over-explaining** | Briefing becomes 90+ seconds | Cut. The general's follow-up question is more valuable than your next sentence. Stop at 30 seconds. |
