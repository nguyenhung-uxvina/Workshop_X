## The Three Laws — Final Version

### Law 1: The Deployed Product Law
*A deployed product is worth infinitely more than a perfect analysis. You have deployed products. Scale them.*

The Analyst Trap for Workshop X is no longer "build something physical" — that's done. The refined trap is: spending time analyzing the next product instead of scaling the current ones. BB-01 deployed to one unit → BB-01 deployed to every infantry training range in Vietnam is a scaling problem, not a design problem.

### Law 2: The ACH Recursion Law
*Apply ACH to your own ACH products. Each layer of AI adds a pricing tier.*

BB-01 as hardware → BB-01 with auto-scoring → BB-01 with training analytics → BB-01 as part of integrated SCOREBOARD → SCOREBOARD feeding into AICC. Each recursion layer increases the price point, the margin, the switching cost, and the competitive moat. The ACH principle doesn't just apply to the product — it applies to the business model.

### Law 3: The Trust Compound Law
*Every successful deployment multiplies the probability of the next contract. Protect the deployment success rate above all else.*

Trust in defense is binary and asymmetric: 10 successful deployments build trust linearly; 1 failure can destroy it exponentially. This means quality gates, acceptance testing rigor, and post-deployment support are not costs — they are investments in the trust substrate that enables everything else. The moment Workshop X ships a product that fails in the field, the entire compound stack devalues.
