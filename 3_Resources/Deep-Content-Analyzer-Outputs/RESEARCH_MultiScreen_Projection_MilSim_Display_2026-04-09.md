## STEP 5: ANALYSIS — KEY FINDINGS PER TOPIC

### Topic 1: Academic/Military Specs — FOV, Pixel Density, Screen Configuration

**Key Findings:**

**FOV Requirement (★★★ HIGH — Tier S)**
- DTIC ADA098267 establishes that simulator visual systems must cover the "foveal vision capability over a 4 steradian (sr) field of view" as a baseline requirement
- Minimum horizontal FOV for weapon training: 120° (practical floor for fire control tasks)
- Preferred for C-UAV/sensor operator tasks: 180° horizontal, 40–60° vertical
- 180° horizontal covers peripheral target detection — critical for operators scanning for small UAVs at sea

**Pixel Density / Visual Acuity Threshold (★★★ HIGH — Tier S + Cross-validated)**
- Human 20/20 vision resolves 1 arcminute = 60 pixels per degree (ppd) is the fidelity ceiling
- Military training threshold (from ADA084776): degradation is imperceptible at ≥ 2 arcmin/pixel (equivalent to ≥ 30 ppd)
- Training transfer is not significantly affected below 30 ppd — confirmed by psychophysical studies
- Advanced ADDNS program used >20 million pixels total across 16 synchronized image generators for full fidelity
- Practical floor for weapon trainer: ≥ 15 ppd at screen center (foveal zone) — below this, target recognition degrades measurably
- **For 3-screen 180° FHD system:** At 60° per screen with 1920px horizontal per screen → 32 ppd — meets threshold

**Screen Configuration (★★★ HIGH — Tier A)**
- Wide-View Visual Systems research (A1) confirms 3-screen flat or 3-channel curved projection is the standard for < 200° FOV simulators
- Curved geometry preferred for weapon simulators: eliminates parallax error at seam, preserves angular accuracy
- 3 flat screens at 60° each = 180° but introduces seam shadow artifacts that degrade threat detection
- Curved single-screen (cylindrical) + 3 projectors = best geometry for seamless threat tracking

**Key Numeric Targets Derived from Tier S:**

### Topic 2: Projection vs LED Wall vs VR Headset — Training Transfer Evidence

**Key Findings:**

**Projection (Large Screen) — Preferred for Crew/Operator Training (★★★ HIGH — Tier S/A)**
- DTIC AD1206915 (2022): For tasks requiring shared situational awareness among crew, projection-based systems consistently outperform individual HMD in coordination tasks
- Projection enables natural body orientation and head movement — critical for weapon pointing/tracking tasks
- Projection preserves natural binocular depth cues and stereopsis at distances > 5m
- Decision-making performance in projection-based simulators is comparable to live-fire training (DTIC comparison data)

**VR Headsets — Effective but Task-Specific Limitation (★★ MED — Tier A)**
- VR headsets (DTIC ADA411978, A2): Effective for individual operator familiarization, procedure training, decision-making drills
- Key limitation: Cybersickness (motion sickness in HMD) degrades performance in maritime scenarios — sea-state simulation with vessel motion + HMD visual conflict → high dropout rates
- Pixel density of consumer HMDs (Meta Quest 3: ~25 ppd, PSVR2: ~23 ppd) is below military fidelity threshold for small target detection (UAVs at 300m appear as 2-4 pixel objects)
- Military-grade HMDs (Varjo XR-4: 70 ppd in foveal zone) address resolution but cost $10,000+/unit vs $3-8K/projector
- DTIC conclusion: VR headsets are complementary to projection, NOT replacement for crew trainer

**LED Walls — Brightness Advantage, Fidelity Limitations (★★ MED — Tier B/C)**
- Tier C source (Durway, C1): LED walls achieve 1,000-2,000 nits (vs 50-150 nits for projection) — advantage for daylight simulation
- Critical weakness: Minimum pixel pitch for 2m viewing distance = 0.9mm (P0.9), cost per sqm at this pitch = $5,000-15,000 USD
- For 180° curved LED wall at operator distance 2m: requires ~12 sqm surface → $60K-180K+ for screen only
- Seam uniformity and color consistency across panels degrades at oblique angles >45° — problematic for 180° setup
- Motion blur on fast-moving targets (drones at 100 kph) can be worse on LED panels than laser projectors at low refresh rates

### Topic 3: Short-Throw Projector Specs for Edge-Blended Multi-Screen

**Key Findings (★★★ HIGH — Tier B OEM data, cross-validated):**

**Laser vs Lamp Decision:**
- **Laser (phosphor or RGB): Mandatory for simulation use** — reasons:
  - 30,000-40,000 hour lifespan vs 2,000-4,000 hours for lamp → no color shift mid-deployment
  - Instant on/off, no warm-up → training session flexibility
  - Consistent brightness across life (laser maintains >80% brightness at 20,000 hrs vs lamp at 50% by 2,000 hrs)
  - Better color volume → critical for FLIR simulation (grayscale uniformity) and vegetation/maritime color accuracy
  - DLP laser: Best for military sim — single-chip, no convergence errors, no color wheel artifacts

**Throw Ratio Selection for 180° 3-Screen Setup:**
- 3-screen setup with each screen covering 60° arc
- If operator station depth (projector to screen): 1.5–2.5m → throw ratio 0.5:1 to 0.8:1 required
- Ultra short throw (< 0.4:1): Not recommended — keystone distortion increases, brightness uniformity degrades >15% at edges
- Recommended throw range: **0.5:1 to 0.8:1** (short throw, not ultra-short)

**Brightness Requirements (★★★ HIGH — derived from Tier S luminance specs):**
- DTIC ADA098267 minimum luminance: 34 cd/m² (night/dusk scenes)
- For maritime simulation including daylight haze: target 80-120 cd/m² on screen surface
- At screen size 2m × 1.1m (FHD, 16:9), area = 2.2 sqm
- Required projector output: 80 cd/m² × 2.2 sqm × π (projection efficiency) ÷ 0.9 (screen gain) ≈ **3,500-5,000 ANSI lumens minimum**
- With ambient light (windows, operator lights): add 30% buffer → **4,500-6,500 lumens recommended**

**Contrast Ratio (★★★):**
- On-screen contrast (projector native CR) must achieve > 500:1 for night scenes
- For FLIR simulation (grayscale only): native CR > 1,000:1 is essential to render 8-bit grayscale without banding
- DLP laser native CR: 2,000:1 to 3,000,000:1 (BenQ FOFO method vs sequential)
- **Sequential/native CR > 2,000:1 is functional minimum; > 10,000:1 preferred**

- Ocean wave model: Gerstner waves + FFT superposition — physically plausible but not fully validated against oceanographic data at extreme sea states
- For VN maritime context (South China Sea, sea state 2-4 typical): UE5 stock water system is sufficient for training
- Custom additions needed: vessel dynamics (6-DOF response to wave field), wake models for USV/UAV water contact

**EO/IR Sensor Simulation — Specialized Plugins Required (★★★ — Tier A):**

### Topic 5: Day/Night/Thermal (FLIR) Simulation Display Requirements

**Key Findings (★★★ HIGH — Tier S/A):**

**Day Scene Requirements:**
- Luminance target: 100-500 cd/m² (real world overcast to clear day: 10,000-100,000 cd/m²)
- Projectors cannot achieve true daylight — fundamental limitation of projection
- Practical daylight simulation floor: 100 cd/m² on screen → requires ≥6,000 lm projector on 2m wide screen
- Workaround: reduce ambient light in simulator room (blackout curtains), calibrate "visual gain" in scene
- LED wall advantage: 500-2,000 nits on screen — significantly better daylight fidelity

**Night Scene / Starlight:**
- Luminance target: 0.001-0.01 cd/m² (moonless night → moonlit)
- Projectors with native CR > 2,000:1 can represent night scenes adequately
- Room must be light-controlled — any ambient light from operator console degrades night scene contrast
- Recommendation: Use separate "night mode" calibration profile on projector

**Thermal/FLIR Display (★★★ HIGH — most critical for C-UAV mission):**
- FLIR display is fundamentally grayscale (8-bit or 14-bit scaled to 8-bit display)
- Display requirement: high grayscale uniformity — maximum 2% luminance variation across screen
- Contrast between drone (warm, ~35°C) vs sea background (15-20°C): delta-T = 15-20°C → must render as distinguishable gray levels
- **Minimum gray levels to render 1°C thermal resolution:** 256 gray levels / 40°C scene range = 6.4 gray levels per °C → requires 8-bit display minimum (10-bit preferred)
- BenQ/Optoma DLP projectors: 8-bit color depth → adequate for FLIR simulation
- Projector for FLIR mode: disable color management, use grayscale LUT, set contrast to maximum
- On-screen contrast CR > 1,000:1 native mandatory for meaningful FLIR gray-level discrimination

**Multi-Mode Display Switching:**
- Training sequence: Visible (day) → Visible (dusk/night) → FLIR (LWIR) → NV (near-IR)
- Software-controlled mode switching via UE5 rendering pipeline (post-process volume swap)
- Projector must support: instant mode/LUT switch < 1 frame (DLP laser: YES; LCOS lamp: NO)
- Separate projector calibration profiles per mode (day, night, FLIR) stored and recalled via display controller
