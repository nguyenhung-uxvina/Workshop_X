### 7.1 Primary Mnemonic: "BACKUP READY"

**🎯 Target Concept:**
The complete minimum risk design process: from identifying uncertainties to having backup solutions ready.

**🧠 Primary Mnemonic:**
**Type:** Acronym + Story
**Mnemonic:** **"BACKUP READY"** = Balance, Alternatives, Checkpoints, Keep provisions, Uncertainty mapped, Procedures written, Ready solutions, Every day follow-up, Adjust step-by-step, Data-driven, Yield savings

### Component Breakdown:

| Letter | Concept | Vietnamese | Explanation |
|--------|---------|------------|-------------|
| **B** | **Balance** tech & economic risk | Cân bằng rủi ro | Don't over-engineer; cheapest adequate solution |
| **A** | **Alternatives** preserved | Giữ lại phương án thay thế | Keep conceptual phase variants |
| **C** | **Checkpoints** defined | Điểm kiểm tra | When do we decide to switch? |
| **K** | **Keep** provisions in design | Giữ điều khoản dự phòng | Space, interface, structural provisions |
| **U** | **Uncertainty** mapped | Ánh xạ độ không chắc chắn | Know what you don't know |
| **P** | **Procedures** written | Viết quy trình | How to switch to backup |
| **R** | **Ready** solutions developed | Giải pháp sẵn sàng | 60-80% developed backups |
| **E** | **Every** day follow-up | Theo dõi hàng ngày | Systematic field data collection |
| **A** | **Adjust** step-by-step | Điều chỉnh từng bước | Incremental, not binary changes |
| **D** | **Data**-driven decisions | Quyết định dựa trên dữ liệu | Test results trigger actions |
| **Y** | **Yield** savings for testing | Dành tiết kiệm cho kiểm tra | Use cost savings for validation |

### 💡 Memory Reinforcement Story:

Imagine a Vietnamese general preparing for a military operation. He knows the enemy might counterattack from multiple directions—he can't be certain.

**"General BACKUP orders his troops to be READY"**

The general doesn't build the heaviest fortifications everywhere (too expensive, too slow). Instead, he:
- **B**alances defensive strength with mobility
- Keeps **A**lternative positions scouted
- Establishes **C**heckpoints to trigger reinforcement
- **K**eeps supply lines provisioned for changes
- Has **U**ncertainty about enemy movements mapped
- **P**rocedures for rapid redeployment written
- **R**eady reserve forces positioned
- **E**very day sends scouts for follow-up intelligence
- **A**djusts positions step-by-step as intelligence arrives
- Makes **D**ata-driven decisions (actual sightings, not assumptions)
- **Y**ields some front-line troops to maintain mobile reserves

When the enemy attacks from an unexpected direction, the general doesn't panic—his BACKUP is READY.

### 7.2 Secondary Mnemonic: "CÂN BẰNG RỦI RO" (Dual-Risk Balance)

**🎯 Target Concept:**
The balance between technical risk and economic risk—the core insight of minimum risk design.

**🧠 Mnemonic (Rhyme):**

```
Kỹ thuật rủi ro, kinh tế rủi ro,
Cân bằng hai bên, đừng lệch một phe.
Rẻ nhưng đủ tốt, thử nghiệm nhiều hơn,
Đắt quá an toàn, thị trường không chờ!
```

**Translation:**
Technical risk, economic risk,
Balance both sides, don't lean one way.
Cheap but good enough, test more often,
Too expensive and safe, the market won't wait!

### 📖 Component Breakdown:

| Line | Meaning | Design Principle |
|------|---------|-----------------|
| Line 1 | Two types of risk exist | Acknowledge both technical AND economic risk |
| Line 2 | Must balance, not eliminate one | Over-focus on either causes problems |
| Line 3 | Affordable solution allows more testing | Savings enable iteration and learning |
| Line 4 | Over-safe = uncompetitive | Market punishes over-engineering |

### 💡 Memory Reinforcement:
Visualize a traditional Vietnamese balance scale (cân đĩa). One dish holds "Kỹ thuật" (a gear/machine), the other holds "Kinh tế" (money/coins). The scale must stay level—if you pile too much weight on either side, the whole thing tips over and crashes.

### 7.3 Process Mnemonic: "FIVE PROVISIONS" (Space for Alternatives)

**🎯 Target Concept:**
The five types of provisions that must be built into a design to accept backup solutions.

**🧠 Mnemonic (Method of Loci - Military Base Tour):**

Imagine walking through a Vietnamese military maintenance depot:

1. **Entrance Gate (INTERFACE):** The gate has universal vehicle connectors—any truck, APC, or tank can plug in for diagnostics. Same connectors, different vehicles.

2. **Vehicle Bay (SPACE):** The maintenance bay is oversized—it can fit the largest vehicle or multiple smaller ones. Space reserved, not wasted.

3. **Structural Lift (STRUCTURAL):** The hydraulic lift has attachment points for any vehicle—multiple lift points, selectable configurations.

4. **Calibration Room (ADJUSTMENT):** Tools have adjustable settings—torque wrenches with variable limits, multimeters with selectable ranges.

5. **Records Office (DOCUMENTATION):** Complete manuals for every vehicle type, with switching procedures clearly marked.

### 📖 Provision Types Summary:

| Location in Story | Provision Type | What to Design |
|-------------------|----------------|----------------|
| Gate | Interface | Standardized connectors, mounting patterns |
| Bay | Space | Volume reservation, routing paths |
| Lift | Structural | Attachment points, reinforcement options |
| Calibration Room | Adjustment | Variable parameters, tunable settings |
| Records Office | Documentation | Procedures, specifications, decision criteria |

### 💡 Memory Reinforcement:
Walk through this mental maintenance depot every time you review a design. At each stop, ask: "Does my design have this provision?"

1. Name the five provisions in order (gate to records).

### 7.4 Example Application Mnemonic: "STUFFING BOX STORY"

**🎯 Target Concept:**
Pahl & Beitz Example 1 (stuffing box cooling)—a model for minimum risk design.

**🧠 Mnemonic (Story):**

A Vietnamese ship engineer faces a hot stuffing box problem. The packing seals are overheating as the shaft spins faster.

**"Two cooling paths, one housing"**

The engineer doesn't know if natural convection (simple) will work or if forced convection (complex) is needed. What does he do?

1. **Designs ONE housing** that accepts EITHER cooling system
2. **Builds with simple** natural convection first
3. **Keeps forced convection** parts ready (fan, ducting)
4. **Tests at sea** to find actual performance
5. **Upgrades only if needed** (and knows exactly where the limit is)

The ship sails. The simple cooling works for 80% of conditions. Only the South China Sea summer requires the upgrade—which installs in 2 days because the housing was ready.

**Key phrase:** "ONE housing, TWO options, TEST first, UPGRADE only if needed"

### 💡 Memory Reinforcement:
Whenever designing a thermal management system, recall the stuffing box story. Ask: "Can my housing accept both simple and complex cooling without modification?"
