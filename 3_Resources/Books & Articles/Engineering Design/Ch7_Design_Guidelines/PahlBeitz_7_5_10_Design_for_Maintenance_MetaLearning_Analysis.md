## Skill 1: Engineering Feynman Explanation

### 🎓 DESIGN FOR MAINTENANCE - Giải Thích Đơn Giản

### 💡 60-Second Explanation

**Design for Maintenance** (DfMt) is designing products so they can be kept working properly throughout their life with minimum effort and cost. Every machine eventually wears out, gets dirty, or needs adjustment - DfMt ensures these inevitable needs can be addressed safely, quickly, and affordably.

According to Pahl & Beitz, maintenance involves three activities:
- **Service** = keeping things running smoothly (oiling, cleaning, refilling)
- **Inspection** = checking if things are still OK (measuring wear, testing function)
- **Repair** = fixing things that broke or wore out (replacing parts, adjusting)

The best design goal? **No maintenance needed at all.** Only when that's impossible should you design for easy maintenance.

### 🏠 Everyday Analogy: Your House

Think of maintaining your house:

| House Maintenance | Engineering DfMt Equivalent |
|-------------------|----------------------------|
| Changing air filters monthly | **Service** (scheduled, keeps things running) |
| Checking smoke detector batteries | **Inspection** (verify intended condition) |
| Replacing broken window | **Failure repair** (fix after it breaks) |
| Replacing roof shingles at 20 years | **Interval preventive repair** (time-based) |
| Fixing leak when meter spikes | **Condition preventive repair** (sensor-triggered) |

A well-designed house has:
- Easy access to filters (you can reach them)
- Battery compartment that opens without tools
- Standard window sizes (replacement available)
- Durable materials (less frequent repair needed)

A poorly designed house hides the air filter behind a wall, requires a ladder to check smoke detectors, and uses custom window sizes nobody stocks.

### 🎯 Defense Training System Example: 12.7mm Remote Controlled Weapon Station (RCWS)

| DfMt Principle | RCWS Application |
|----------------|------------------|
| **Service** | Barrel cleaning after X rounds; lubricating traverse mechanism; refilling hydraulic fluid |
| **Inspection** | Checking barrel wear with go/no-go gauge; testing sensor calibration; verifying ammunition feed alignment |
| **Repair (Failure)** | Replacing broken camera cable (unpredictable failure) |
| **Repair (Interval)** | Replacing barrel every 10,000 rounds (time/usage-based) |
| **Repair (Condition)** | Replacing seal when hydraulic pressure drops 10% (sensor-triggered) |
| **Minimize maintenance** | Self-lubricating bushings; sealed electronics; modular LRUs |
| **Easy access** | Hinged cover panels; tool-free latches; adequate clearance |
| **Error prevention** | Keyed connectors (can't connect wrong); color-coded hydraulic lines |

### ❓ Verification Questions

1. **Basic recall:** What are the three activities that constitute maintenance according to DIN 31051?
2. **Application:** A training grenade's electronic fuze requires battery replacement. Is this Service, Inspection, or Repair?
3. **Deeper understanding:** Why does Pahl & Beitz prioritize "no maintenance needed" over "easy maintenance"?
4. **Edge case:** A system has perfect maintenance access but dangerous maintenance procedures. Does this satisfy DfMt? Why not?

### ✅ Answers

1. Service, Inspection, Repair
2. Service (maintaining intended condition by refilling/replacing consumable)
3. Because even "easy" maintenance has costs (downtime, labor, spare parts, potential errors). Zero maintenance is always better if achievable. Additionally, maintenance introduces opportunities for human error.
4. No - "design for maintenance should never compromise safety." Ergonomic and safety requirements must be satisfied, not just accessibility.

### ⚠️ Common Misconceptions

| ❌ Wrong Belief | ✅ Correct Understanding |
|----------------|-------------------------|
| "DfMt = making maintenance easy" | DfMt = eliminating maintenance need first, then making unavoidable maintenance easy |
| "More maintenance access = better design" | Sometimes adding access panels adds weight, cost, failure points; balance needed |
| "Preventive repair is always better than failure repair" | Failure repair is acceptable when failures are unpredictable AND don't cause danger |
| "Maintenance is separate from safety" | DfMt must work WITH safety - never compromise safety for maintenance convenience |
| "Interval repair is simpler than condition repair" | Interval repair may replace parts unnecessarily; condition repair more efficient but requires sensors |

### Chunk 1: Goals and Terminology
**Duration:** 45 minutes | **Difficulty:** ⭐⭐ (Intermediate)

**Prerequisites:** None

**Core Concepts (7 items):**
1. Definition of maintenance (DIN 31051)
2. Why systems deteriorate (wear, corrosion, contamination, embrittlement)
3. Deviation from intended vs. actual condition
4. Impact on functionality, economy, safety
5. Designer's influence on maintainability
6. Relationship to safety, ergonomics, assembly
7. Maintenance cost as lifecycle consideration

**Explanation:**

Technical systems inevitably degrade. Wear reduces surface quality, corrosion attacks materials, contamination clogs passages, and time-dependent properties like embrittlement reduce strength. The "intended condition" defined by design specifications gradually diverges from "actual condition" in the field.

This matters because:
- **Functionality degrades** - system no longer performs as required
- **Economy suffers** - unexpected repairs cost more than planned maintenance
- **Safety compromises** - worn parts may fail dangerously

Designers don't just design the initial product - they determine how easy or hard it will be to maintain throughout its life. The choice of principle solution (conceptual design) and embodiment features strongly influence:
- What maintenance will be needed
- How often
- How difficult
- How expensive

**Defense Application: Target USV**

A Target USV operating in seawater experiences:
- **Wear:** Propeller bearing wear from continuous operation
- **Corrosion:** Hull deterioration from salt spray
- **Contamination:** Fuel system fouling from biofuel degradation
- **Embrittlement:** Plastic components becoming brittle from UV exposure

Without maintenance consideration during design:
- Functionality: USV speed drops as propeller efficiency degrades
- Economy: Unexpected emergency repairs in field more expensive than planned depot maintenance
- Safety: Corroded safety systems (emergency stop, collision avoidance) may fail

### Chunk 2: Maintenance Measures (Service, Inspection, Repair)
**Duration:** 60 minutes | **Difficulty:** ⭐⭐⭐ (Intermediate-Advanced)

**Prerequisites:** Chunk 1 completed

**Core Concepts (6 items):**
1. Service - maintaining intended condition
2. Inspection - monitoring/assessing actual condition
3. Repair - recovering intended condition
4. Factors determining measure type/extent (system type, function, availability, reliability, danger)
5. Timing strategies (fixed time, operating hours, load intensity)
6. Service activities (refilling, lubricating, conserving, cleaning)

**Explanation:**

**SERVICE** keeps the system in its intended condition through:
- **Refilling** consumables (lubricants, coolants, fuel)
- **Lubricating** moving parts to reduce wear
- **Conserving** surfaces against corrosion (applying protective coatings)
- **Cleaning** to remove contamination

The goal is preventing deterioration before it happens.

**INSPECTION** determines the current actual condition by:
- **Monitoring** parameters (temperature, pressure, vibration)
- **Measuring** wear indicators (thickness, clearance, alignment)
- **Testing** function (does it still work correctly?)
- **Assessing** whether maintenance is needed

Inspection tells you IF action is needed and WHEN.

**REPAIR** recovers the intended condition by:
- **Adjusting** components back to specification
- **Replacing** worn or damaged parts
- **Overhauling** assemblies through disassembly, cleaning, replacing, reassembly
- **Restoring** surfaces (re-machining, re-coating)

**What determines the approach?**

| Factor | Effect on Maintenance Approach |
|--------|-------------------------------|
| System type | Complex systems need more structured maintenance |
| Intended function | Critical functions need higher reliability maintenance |
| Required availability | High-availability systems need predictive maintenance |
| Desired reliability | High reliability = more preventive measures |
| Potential dangers | Safety-critical systems cannot accept failure repair |

**Defense Application: LOMAH System**

| Measure Type | LOMAH Application |
|--------------|-------------------|
| **Service** | Clean sensor lenses weekly; lubricate motorized target retrieval monthly; replace desiccant in electronics enclosures |
| **Inspection** | Check sensor calibration before each training session; verify target position accuracy; test communication links |
| **Repair** | Replace worn target carrier rails; recalibrate sensors that drift; replace damaged acoustic panels |

**Timing for LOMAH maintenance:**
- Fixed time: Annual sensor recalibration (calendar-based)
- Operating hours: Target carrier mechanism after 10,000 cycles
- Load intensity: Sensor cleaning after high-dust exercises

### Chunk 3: Repair Strategies (Failure vs. Preventive)
**Duration:** 60 minutes | **Difficulty:** ⭐⭐⭐ (Advanced)

**Prerequisites:** Chunks 1-2 completed

**Core Concepts (8 items):**
1. Failure repair - repair after failure occurs
2. Conditions for failure repair (unpredictable failure, no danger)
3. Preventive repair - repair before failure occurs
4. Interval-based preventive repair (fixed time/distance/operations)
5. Condition-based preventive repair (measured parameters)
6. Combining interval and condition strategies
7. Choosing appropriate strategy
8. Failure probability and reliability prediction

**Explanation:**

**FAILURE REPAIR** means waiting until something breaks, then fixing it.

When is this acceptable?
- Failures **cannot be predicted accurately** (random failure pattern)
- Failures **do not cause danger** (safety not compromised)

When is this NOT acceptable?
- Production plant (disruption too costly)
- Function must be fulfilled (mission-critical)
- Danger is involved (safety-critical)

Example: Car windscreen shattering - unpredictable, not dangerous (driver can still see enough to pull over safely).

**PREVENTIVE REPAIR** means replacing/adjusting BEFORE failure occurs.

**Interval-based:** Fixed schedule regardless of actual condition
- After fixed **time** (oil change every 6 months)
- After specific **distance** (oil change every 10,000 km)
- After set **operations** (barrel replacement every 10,000 rounds)

Advantage: Simple to schedule, no sensors needed
Disadvantage: May replace parts that still have life remaining

**Condition-based:** Triggered by measured parameters
- Temperature reached threshold
- Pressure dropped below minimum
- Wear measured beyond limit
- Vibration increased beyond acceptable

Advantage: Parts used to full life, prevents unnecessary replacement
Disadvantage: Requires sensors, monitoring systems, more complex

**Combination Strategy:**

Example from text - Power station:
- Time interval repair for base load components (safety margin)
- Condition repair for components that might last multiple intervals (efficiency)

**Defense Application: Target UAV**

| Component | Repair Strategy | Rationale |
|-----------|-----------------|-----------|
| Flight control surfaces | Condition-based (visual inspection + play measurement) | Wear varies with flight intensity |
| Engine | Interval-based (replace at 500 flight hours) | Failure prediction uncertain, dangerous failure mode |
| Radio transponder | Failure repair | Solid-state electronics, random failure, safe failure mode (loss of tracking) |
| Landing gear tires | Condition-based (tread depth measurement) | Visible wear indicator, predictable wear |
| Pyrotechnic kill switch | Interval-based (replace annually) | Chemical aging unpredictable, safety-critical |

### Chunk 4: Design Priorities for Maintenance
**Duration:** 45 minutes | **Difficulty:** ⭐⭐⭐ (Advanced)

**Prerequisites:** Chunks 1-3 completed

**Core Concepts (7 items):**
1. Priority 1: Design for NO maintenance (eliminate need)
2. Priority 2: Design for MINIMAL maintenance (reduce need)
3. Priority 3: Design for EASY maintenance (simplify what remains)
4. Maintenance requirements in requirements list
5. Selecting easily-maintained variants
6. Components with similar life expectancies
7. Safety cannot be compromised for maintenance

**Explanation:**

Pahl & Beitz establishes a clear **hierarchy of design priorities:**

**FIRST PRIORITY: Eliminate maintenance need entirely**

The ideal is "complete freedom from the need for service" through:
- Components with identical operational lives
- Built-in reliability (no wear-out mechanisms)
- Inherent safety (no degradation affects safety)

If this cannot be achieved or is too costly, then:

**SECOND PRIORITY: Reduce maintenance substantially**

- Fewer maintenance actions required
- Longer intervals between maintenance
- Less critical consequences of delayed maintenance

If full elimination isn't possible, then:

**THIRD PRIORITY: Make unavoidable maintenance easy**

Only when maintenance cannot be eliminated should effort focus on:
- Simplifying service procedures
- Making inspection straightforward
- Enabling quick repair

**Critical constraint:** Maintenance design must NEVER compromise safety.

**Maintenance aims (from text):**
1. Prevent damage and increase reliability
2. Avoid errors during disassembly, reassembly, start-up
3. Simplify service procedures
4. Make servicing results checkable
5. Simplify inspection procedures

**Defense Application: Machine Gun Mount System**

| Design Priority | Mount System Implementation |
|-----------------|----------------------------|
| **Eliminate maintenance** | Use self-lubricating bushings (no greasing needed); sealed bearings (no dirt ingress); corrosion-resistant materials (no rust treatment) |
| **Reduce maintenance** | Fewer moving parts; standardized fasteners; symmetrical design (no "wrong" assembly) |
| **Easy maintenance** | Quick-release pins for traverse lock; hinged covers (no tools to remove); accessible grease points (if lubrication unavoidable) |
| **Safety constraint** | Locking mechanism must function even if maintenance delayed; no pinch points near maintenance access |

**Practice Exercise:**

Redesign a UAV Catapult system applying the three-priority hierarchy:
1. What maintenance can be ELIMINATED through design choices?
2. What maintenance can be REDUCED through better component selection?
3. What maintenance remains unavoidable - how do you make it EASY?
4. What safety constraints limit your maintenance design choices?

### Chunk 5: Technical Measures for Maintainability
**Duration:** 75 minutes | **Difficulty:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (Advanced)

**Prerequisites:** Chunks 1-4 completed

**Core Concepts (9 items):**

**Conceptual phase measures:**
1. Prefer self-balancing and self-adjusting solutions
2. Aim at simplicity and few parts
3. Use standard components
4. Allow easy access
5. Provide for easy disassembly
6. Apply modular principles
7. Use few and similar service/inspection tools

**Embodiment phase (ergonomic rules):**
8. Accessibility (service/inspection/repair locations)
9. Working environment (safety and ergonomic requirements)

**Explanation:**

Technical measures should be considered **early** (conceptual phase) because they affect fundamental design choices.

**Self-balancing/self-adjusting solutions:**
- Systems that automatically compensate for wear or drift
- Example: Self-adjusting brake mechanisms, self-tensioning belt drives
- Eliminates need for periodic adjustment

**Simplicity and few parts:**
- Fewer parts = fewer potential failure points = less maintenance
- Simple mechanisms are easier to understand and service
- Reduces spare parts inventory

**Standard components:**
- Readily available replacements
- Known service procedures
- Documented reliability data
- Lower spare parts cost

**Easy access:**
- Components requiring service are reachable
- Adequate clearance for tools and hands
- Logical arrangement (service points grouped)

**Easy disassembly:**
- Clear sequence for disassembly/reassembly
- Minimal special tools required
- Components can be removed without disturbing others
- Keyed/marked for correct reassembly

**Modular principles:**
- Self-contained modules can be replaced as units
- Line Replaceable Units (LRUs) in defense terminology
- Simplifies field maintenance (swap module, repair later)

**Similar tools:**
- One wrench size fits all fasteners
- Standardized test equipment across systems
- Reduces technician training and tool inventory

**Ergonomic rules for maintenance execution:**
- Service/inspection/repair locations easily accessible
- Working environment follows safety requirements
- Visibility ensured
- Functional processes and supporting measures clear
- Damage localization possible
- Exchange of components easy

**Documentation requirements:**
- DIN 31052: Developing maintenance manuals
- DIN 31054: Determining maintenance intervals
- Clear labeling of service points

**Defense Application: Radar-IR Target Simulation System**

| Technical Measure | Radar-IR Simulator Implementation |
|-------------------|----------------------------------|
| Self-adjusting | Automatic gain control in radar simulator; self-calibrating IR emitter |
| Simplicity | Integrated electronics module (not multiple boards) |
| Standard components | Commercial GPS module; standard RF connectors |
| Easy access | Top-opening enclosure; service panel faces operator |
| Easy disassembly | Captive fasteners (can't lose screws); quick-disconnect cables |
| Modular | Separate radar module, IR module, power module (each replaceable) |
| Similar tools | All fasteners M5 hex; single diagnostic connector |
| Accessibility | All LRUs reachable standing; no crawling under vehicle |
| Environment | Shaded service area specified; adequate lighting |
| Visibility | Status LEDs visible from 2m; clear fault codes on display |

### Chunk 6: Integration into Total Design Concept
**Duration:** 60 minutes | **Difficulty:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (Expert)

**Prerequisites:** Chunks 1-5 completed

**Core Concepts (6 items):**
1. Maintenance as part of total concept
2. Compatibility with functional constraints
3. Compatibility with operational constraints
4. Total cost = purchase + operating + maintenance
5. Related guidelines (safety, ergonomics, assembly)
6. Life cycle perspective

**Explanation:**

Maintenance is not a standalone concern - it must integrate with the **total design concept**.

**Functional compatibility:**
- Maintenance procedures cannot impair system function
- Service points cannot introduce failure modes
- Inspection doesn't require system shutdown if availability critical

**Operational compatibility:**
- Maintenance possible in operational environment
- Procedures match operator skill levels
- Tools/spares available in field conditions
- Time for maintenance fits operational tempo

**Total cost perspective:**
- Purchase cost (initial investment)
- Operating costs (energy, consumables, labor)
- **Maintenance costs** (labor, spare parts, downtime, facilities)

Low purchase cost but high maintenance cost = poor design.
Higher purchase cost but lower lifecycle cost = better design decision.

**Related guidelines integration:**

| Guideline | Maintenance Relationship |
|-----------|-------------------------|
| **Safety** (7.3.3) | Maintenance must not compromise safety; safety-critical components need maintenance access |
| **Ergonomics** (7.5.6) | Maintenance postures, forces, visibility follow ergonomic standards |
| **Assembly** (7.5.9) | Design for assembly = design for disassembly during maintenance |

**Defense Application: Complete Training System Integration**

Consider a V-SMASH (Small Arms Marksmanship Simulator) as total concept:

| Integration Aspect | V-SMASH Maintenance Integration |
|--------------------|---------------------------------|
| **Functional compatibility** | Calibration can happen between training sessions; sensor cleaning doesn't require system disassembly |
| **Operational compatibility** | Maintenance performable by unit armorer with standard tools; no specialized depot required for routine service |
| **Total cost** | Unit cost $15K + Operating $200/year + Maintenance $1K/year = TCO $25K over 10-year life |
| **Safety integration** | Laser safety interlocks remain functional during maintenance; maintenance position doesn't expose technician to laser |
| **Ergonomics integration** | All service points accessible at standing height; labels readable in low light |
| **Assembly integration** | Same procedures for assembly and maintenance disassembly; same tools required |

**Life Cycle Perspective:**

```
CONCEPT → DESIGN → PRODUCTION → OPERATION → MAINTENANCE → DISPOSAL
    ↑                                            ↓
    └──────── FEEDBACK LOOP ────────────────────┘
```

Maintenance experience feeds back into future designs:
- Common failures inform reliability requirements
- Maintenance difficulties inform accessibility requirements
- Costs inform economic design decisions

## Skill 3: Design Review Criteria

### DfMt Design Review Rubric

Use this rubric when reviewing maintenance design aspects of defense training systems.

#### Phase-Appropriate Application

| Design Phase | DfMt Focus | Key Questions |
|--------------|------------|---------------|
| **Task Clarification** | Capture maintenance requirements | Are MTBF/MTTR specified? Are maintenance environment conditions defined? |
| **Conceptual Design** | Choose maintainable principles | Are self-adjusting solutions preferred? Are modular architectures considered? |
| **Embodiment Design** | Implement maintenance features | Are service points accessible? Are components easily replaceable? |
| **Detail Design** | Document maintenance procedures | Are maintenance manuals complete? Are intervals specified? |

#### Scoring Criteria (0-10 scale)

| Criterion | Weight | 0-2 (Poor) | 3-5 (Basic) | 6-8 (Good) | 9-10 (Excellent) |
|-----------|--------|------------|-------------|------------|------------------|
| **Maintenance elimination** | HIGH | No elimination consideration | Some attempts at maintenance-free | Most common maintenance eliminated | Near-complete maintenance freedom |
| **Maintenance reduction** | HIGH | No reduction from baseline | 20-40% reduction | 40-60% reduction | >60% reduction |
| **Access design** | HIGH | Critical points inaccessible | Most points accessible with difficulty | All points accessible with tools | All points accessible quickly, tool-free where possible |
| **Modular design** | MEDIUM | No modularity | Some modular elements | Clear LRU structure | Complete plug-and-play modularity |
| **Standard components** | MEDIUM | All custom parts | 50% standard | 75% standard | >90% standard |
| **Tool commonality** | LOW | Many special tools required | Some special tools | Standard tools + 1-2 special | Standard tools only |
| **Documentation** | MEDIUM | No maintenance docs | Basic procedures | Complete procedures with intervals | Full procedures + troubleshooting + parts list |
| **Safety integration** | CRITICAL | Safety compromised | Safety maintained minimally | Safety explicitly addressed | Safety enhanced through maintenance design |

## Skill 12: Targeted Drill Exercises

### Drill Set: Design for Maintenance

#### Drill 1: Maintenance Activity Classification (Pattern Recognition)
**Difficulty:** ⭐⭐ (Developing)  
**Duration:** 15 minutes  
**Weakness addressed:** Cannot correctly classify Service vs. Inspection vs. Repair

**Instructions:**
Classify each activity as Service (S), Inspection (I), or Repair (R). Write your answer before checking.

**Problems:**

1. Cleaning salt spray residue from a Target USV's deck sensors
2. Measuring barrel wear on a 12.7mm RCWS with a bore gauge
3. Replacing a burned-out LED indicator on a LOMAH control panel
4. Lubricating the traverse mechanism on a Machine Gun Mount
5. Testing communication link integrity on a Tethered Drone
6. Adjusting the zero of a Small Arms Simulator laser emitter
7. Replacing desiccant packs in an electronics enclosure
8. Checking battery voltage on a Training Grenade simulator
9. Replacing a cracked camera lens on a Target UAV
10. Refilling hydraulic fluid reservoir on an RCWS

**Model Answers:**

1. **S** - Service: Cleaning maintains intended condition (prevents contamination degradation)
2. **I** - Inspection: Monitoring/assessing actual condition (measuring wear state)
3. **R** - Repair: Recovering intended condition (replacing failed component)
4. **S** - Service: Lubricating maintains intended condition (prevents wear)
5. **I** - Inspection: Assessing actual condition (is link working?)
6. **R** - Repair: Recovering intended condition (zero has drifted, restoring accuracy)
7. **S** - Service: Maintaining intended condition (moisture control)
8. **I** - Inspection: Assessing actual condition (is battery adequate?)
9. **R** - Repair: Recovering intended condition (replacing damaged part)
10. **S** - Service: Refilling maintains intended condition

**Scoring:** 9-10 correct = Mastered; 7-8 = Developing; <7 = Repeat drill

#### Drill 2: Repair Strategy Selection (Application)
**Difficulty:** ⭐⭐⭐ (Proficient)  
**Duration:** 25 minutes  
**Weakness addressed:** Cannot select appropriate repair strategy based on predictability and danger

**Instructions:**
For each component/failure mode, determine the appropriate repair strategy:
- **FR** = Failure Repair (repair after failure)
- **IP** = Interval Preventive (fixed time/usage)
- **CP** = Condition Preventive (sensor/measurement triggered)

Justify your choice based on: (1) Predictability of failure, (2) Danger level if failure occurs.

**Problems:**

1. **Target UAV - Propeller blade fatigue crack**
   - Predictability: ?
   - Danger: ?
   - Strategy: ?
   - Justification:

2. **LOMAH - Acoustic sensor microphone failure**
   - Predictability: ?
   - Danger: ?
   - Strategy: ?
   - Justification:

3. **RCWS - Barrel erosion**
   - Predictability: ?
   - Danger: ?
   - Strategy: ?
   - Justification:

4. **Training Grenade - Pyrotechnic charge degradation**
   - Predictability: ?
   - Danger: ?
   - Strategy: ?
   - Justification:

5. **Small Arms Simulator - Laser diode degradation**
   - Predictability: ?
   - Danger: ?
   - Strategy: ?
   - Justification:

**Model Answers:**

1. **Target UAV - Propeller blade**
   - Predictability: LOW (fatigue cracks difficult to predict exactly)
   - Danger: HIGH (blade failure causes crash, potential injury)
   - Strategy: **IP** (Interval Preventive) - Replace after X flight hours
   - Justification: Danger is high → must be preventive. Prediction uncertain → use conservative interval.

2. **LOMAH - Acoustic sensor**
   - Predictability: LOW (solid-state electronics, random failure)
   - Danger: LOW (system continues with one sensor, no safety hazard)
   - Strategy: **FR** (Failure Repair) - Replace when fails
   - Justification: Not dangerous + unpredictable → acceptable to repair after failure.

3. **RCWS - Barrel erosion**
   - Predictability: HIGH (wear correlates with rounds fired)
   - Danger: HIGH (eroded barrel affects accuracy, potential catastrophic failure)
   - Strategy: **CP** (Condition Preventive) with IP backup - Measure wear, replace at threshold OR at max rounds
   - Justification: Predictable + dangerous → condition monitoring ideal, interval as backup.

4. **Training Grenade - Pyrotechnic**
   - Predictability: MEDIUM (chemical aging time-dependent but variable)
   - Danger: HIGH (pyrotechnic malfunction dangerous)
   - Strategy: **IP** (Interval Preventive) - Replace at shelf life date
   - Justification: Danger high → must be preventive. Time-dependent → interval approach.

5. **Small Arms Simulator - Laser**
   - Predictability: HIGH (laser output measurable, gradual degradation)
   - Danger: LOW (degraded laser = inaccurate training, not dangerous)
   - Strategy: **CP** (Condition Preventive) - Measure output, replace below threshold
   - Justification: Predictable by measurement → condition-based. Not safety-critical → can monitor.

#### Drill 3: Design Priority Application (Deep Reasoning)
**Difficulty:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (Expert)  
**Duration:** 35 minutes  
**Weakness addressed:** Jumps to "easy maintenance" without considering elimination/reduction

**Instructions:**
For the given system, apply the DfMt priority hierarchy. For each maintenance need identified, determine:
- Can it be ELIMINATED? If yes, how?
- If not, can it be REDUCED? If yes, how?
