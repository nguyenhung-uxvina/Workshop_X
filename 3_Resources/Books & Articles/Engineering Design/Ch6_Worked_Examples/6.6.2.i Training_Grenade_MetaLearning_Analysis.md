# SKILL 1: FEYNMAN TECHNIQUE - Simple Explanations

## 1.1 ELI12 Explanation (Explain Like I'm 12)

"A training grenade is like a realistic toy that soldiers use to practice throwing real grenades safely. It looks exactly like a real grenade, weighs the same (about as heavy as a small apple), and works the same way - you pull out a pin, throw it, and after a few seconds it makes a loud 'POP' and maybe some smoke. But here's the important part: unlike a real grenade that explodes and sends metal pieces flying everywhere (which would hurt or kill people), a training grenade just makes noise. It's like the difference between a real gun and a cap gun that just makes sound.

Soldiers need to practice because throwing a grenade is scary and requires skill. You have to hold it right, pull the pin correctly, throw accurately, and take cover quickly. If you've never done it before and your first time is with a real grenade, you might make a deadly mistake. So they practice hundreds of times with training grenades until it becomes second nature. The training grenade 'fools' their brain into thinking it's real (same weight, same feel, same delay, same loud bang) but can't actually hurt anyone."

## 1.2 ELI-Engineer Explanation

"Training grenades solve the fundamental contradiction between training realism and safety. A live M67 fragmentation grenade has specific handling characteristics that determine throwing range and technique: 400g mass, specific center of gravity, 22N pin pull force, 4-5 second M213 fuze delay. These parameters create proprioceptive and kinesthetic feedback that soldiers must internalize for muscle memory.

The design challenge is replicating these exact parameters while eliminating the lethal payload (180g of Composition B explosive, ~1000 fragments). The solution involves:
1. **Body**: Steel or polymer shell with identical geometry (sphere Ø57mm + neck), weight matching via ballast
2. **Fuze Simulator**: Identical mechanism (striker spring, delay element) but with pyrotechnic report instead of detonator
3. **Output**: 140-167 dB audible signal + optional flash/smoke for stress inoculation

Advanced systems (Sim Grenade™) add proximity sensors for automatic casualty assessment in force-on-force training, solving the 'subjective adjudication' problem where instructors must manually determine hits."

### Core Mnemonics

**"NẶNG-GIỐNG-ỔN-AN"** (Heavy-Same-Stable-Safe) - Four Design Pillars:
- **NẶNG**: Weight must match live grenade (400g ±20g)
- **GIỐNG**: Same handling feel (pin, lever, delay)
- **ỔN**: Stable, predictable function (reliable ignition)
- **AN**: Absolute safety (zero fragmentation)

**"RÚT-NÉM-CHỜ-NỔ"** (Pull-Throw-Wait-Bang) - Operating Sequence:
- **RÚT**: Pull safety pin (rút chốt)
- **NÉM**: Throw grenade (ném lựu đạn)
- **CHỜ**: Wait for delay (chờ trễ)
- **NỔ**: Audible report (tiếng nổ)

**"THÂN-NGÒI-TIÊU"** (Body-Fuze-Consumable) - Three Component Categories:
- **THÂN**: Body (reusable 500+ times)
- **NGÒI**: Fuze mechanism (reusable 50-200 times)
- **TIÊU**: Consumables (single use: blank, pyrotechnic)

**"ÂM-SÁNG-KHÓI-ĐIỂM"** (Sound-Light-Smoke-Score) - Four Output Types:
- **ÂM**: Audible report (140-167 dB)
- **SÁNG**: Flash (optional, for stress inoculation)
- **KHÓI**: Smoke (marks impact point)
- **ĐIỂM**: Scoring (electronic, for MILES/Sim Grenade)

### Formula Mnemonic

**"CHI-PHÍ = THÂN + SỐ × TIÊU"** (Cost = Body + Count × Consumable):
> Total Training Cost = Initial Cost + (Number of Throws × Consumable Cost)

For steel body ($80) with blank cartridges ($0.10/throw):
- 100 throws: $80 + 100×$0.10 = $90 ($0.90/throw)
- 1000 throws: $80 + 1000×$0.10 = $180 ($0.18/throw)
- 5000 throws: $80 + 5000×$0.10 = $580 ($0.12/throw)

This illustrates the **economies of scale** - high-reuse bodies amortize initial cost.

## 2.3 E-M-S Flow Classification

**Primary Flow**: ENERGY
```
Chemical Energy (blank/pyrotechnic)
    ↓ ignition
Thermal Energy (combustion)
    ↓ expansion
Acoustic Energy (pressure wave = sound)
    ↓ propagation
Sensory Input (to trainees, 140-167 dB)
```

**Secondary Flow**: ENERGY
```
Mechanical Energy (compressed spring)
    ↓ release
Kinetic Energy (striker movement)
    ↓ impact
Chemical Energy (primer ignition)
    ↓ chain reaction
```

**Tertiary Flow**: MATERIAL
```
Consumable (blank cartridge or pyrotechnic)
    ↓ detonation
Residue (spent casing, smoke particles)
    ↓ disposal
Replacement (new consumable inserted)
```

**Classification**: **APPARATUS** (Energy-dominant with material transformation)
- Unlike MACHINE (continuous energy conversion, e.g., Tethered Drone power system)
- Material (consumable) is transformed, not just transported
- Energy conversion is the primary function (chemical → acoustic)

# SKILL 3: DESIGN REVIEW MENTOR - Critical Issues Analysis

## 3.1 Top 10 Critical Design Issues

### R-01: Weight Matching Accuracy (CRITICAL)
**Issue**: If training grenade weight differs from live grenade by >5%, muscle memory transfers incorrectly
**Requirement**: D: 400g ±20g (5% tolerance for M67), or 310g ±15g for RGD-5
**Verification**: Weighing with calibrated scale, statistical sampling (n≥10)
**Mitigation**: Internal ballast (lead shot or steel slugs) in polymer bodies; precision machining for steel bodies

### R-02: Zero Fragmentation Guarantee (CRITICAL)
**Issue**: ANY fragment projection makes system unsuitable for restricted ranges
**Requirement**: D: Zero metal fragments capable of penetration at any distance
**Verification**: High-speed video of detonation, target paper at 1m distance
**Mitigation**: Contained pyrotechnic chamber; ductile body material; flash powder <1g

### R-03: Delay Time Consistency (HIGH)
**Issue**: Inconsistent delay creates unpredictable training (too short = danger, too long = unrealistic)
**Requirement**: D: 3-5 seconds ±0.5 seconds (matching live fuze variability)
**Verification**: 50+ test firings, statistical analysis of delay distribution
**Mitigation**: Quality-controlled pyrotechnic composition; electronic timer alternative

### R-04: Impact Survival (HIGH)
**Issue**: Body must survive impact without cracking (reusability requirement)
**Requirement**: D: Survive 2m drop onto concrete, 100+ cycles minimum
**Verification**: Drop testing per MIL-STD-810H Method 516.8
**Mitigation**: Steel body (inherently durable); polymer with reinforced sections

### R-05: Pin Pull Force (MEDIUM)
**Issue**: Too easy = accidental arming; too hard = training artifact
**Requirement**: D: 5-10 lbs (22-44N), matching live grenade specification
**Verification**: Force gauge testing, multiple pin insertions
**Mitigation**: Precision machining of pin/hole interface; spring-loaded detent

### R-06: Sound Level Adequacy (MEDIUM)
**Issue**: Too quiet = insufficient stress inoculation; too loud = hearing damage risk
**Requirement**: D: ≥130 dB at 5m (realistic), W: 140-167 dB at 1m (stress inoculation)
**Verification**: Sound level meter at standard distances
**Mitigation**: Calibrated pyrotechnic charge; ear protection mandatory in SOPs

### R-07: Reusability Economics (MEDIUM)
**Issue**: High initial cost is acceptable only if per-throw cost is low
**Requirement**: W: Cost per throw <$0.50 for volume training
**Verification**: Lifecycle cost analysis over 1000 throws
**Mitigation**: High-reuse body (500+ cycles); cheap consumables (blank cartridges)

### R-08: Field Resetability (MEDIUM)
**Issue**: Complex reset procedures reduce training throughput
**Requirement**: W: Reset time <30 seconds with no special tools
**Verification**: Timed reset by untrained personnel
**Mitigation**: Tool-free fuze attachment; pre-loaded consumable cartridges

### R-09: Environmental Durability (LOW)
**Issue**: Training occurs in varied conditions (rain, heat, humidity, dust)
**Requirement**: W: Operational -10°C to +50°C, 95% RH, light rain
**Verification**: Environmental testing per MIL-STD-810H (temperature, humidity)
**Mitigation**: Sealed pyrotechnic compartment; corrosion-resistant materials (stainless steel)

### R-10: Visual Distinctiveness (LOW)
**Issue**: Training grenades must NOT be confused with live grenades
**Requirement**: D: Clearly marked as training (bright color, markings)
**Verification**: Visual inspection, color matching to training standards (typically orange or blue)
**Mitigation**: Full-body paint (orange), permanent markings "TRAINING ONLY"

## 3.3 Common Design Errors

**Error 1**: Specifying weight tolerance too loose (>10%)
- Impact: Inconsistent throwing range, poor muscle memory transfer
- Prevention: Tighten tolerance to ±5%, include ballast adjustment procedure

**Error 2**: Using brittle body material (cast iron, rigid polymer)
- Impact: Cracking on impact, potential fragment hazard
- Prevention: Use ductile steel or reinforced polymer, impact testing

**Error 3**: Oversizing pyrotechnic charge
- Impact: Excessive blast risk, hearing damage, fire hazard
- Prevention: Flash powder <1g, contained chamber, ear protection required

**Error 4**: Neglecting fuze reliability
- Impact: Misfires (training interruption), hangfires (safety hazard)
- Prevention: Redundant ignition, quality-controlled pyrotechnics, misfire procedures

**Error 5**: Ignoring reset ergonomics
- Impact: Low training throughput, instructor frustration
- Prevention: Tool-free design, <30 second reset, pre-loaded cartridges

# SKILL 6: CONCEPT EVALUATION ASSISTANT - VDI 2225

## 6.1 Five Concept Variants

### V1: Basic Pyrotechnic (Nammo-type)
- **Body**: Steel, machined, M67 profile
- **Fuze**: Reusable striker + pyrotechnic delay
- **Output**: Audible pop (~120 dB)
- **Reusability**: Body 500+, fuze 100+
- **Initial Cost**: $80-100
- **Per-throw Cost**: $0.50 (training fuze)
- **Indigenous**: 80% (body local, fuze components import)

### V2: Enhanced Pyrotechnic (FBSS-type)
- **Body**: Steel or polymer, M67 profile
- **Fuze**: Reusable striker + FBSS-Mini insert
- **Output**: Flash + Bang + Smoke (167 dB)
- **Reusability**: Body 500+, fuze 100+
- **Initial Cost**: $100-150
- **Per-throw Cost**: $5-8 (FBSS insert)
- **Indigenous**: 60% (pyrotechnic insert import)

### V3: Electronic Smart (Sim Grenade-type)
- **Body**: Polymer with padding, M67 profile
- **Fuze**: Electronic timer + proximity sensors
- **Output**: LED flash + speaker (100 dB) + wireless data
- **Reusability**: Body 1000+, electronics 500+
- **Initial Cost**: $500-800
- **Per-throw Cost**: $0.10 (battery/maintenance)
- **Indigenous**: 50% (sensors/electronics import)

### V4: Low-Cost Blank Cartridge
- **Body**: Steel, machined, RGD-5 profile (Vietnamese standard)
- **Fuze**: Simple striker + .22 blank
- **Output**: Audible bang (~140 dB)
- **Reusability**: Body 500+, fuze 200+
- **Initial Cost**: $50-70
- **Per-throw Cost**: $0.10 (.22 blank)
- **Indigenous**: 90% (blanks available locally)

### V5: Eco-Friendly Biodegradable (K417-type)
- **Body**: PLA polymer + clay, dissolvable
- **Fuze**: Simple striker + small pyrotechnic
- **Output**: Small pop (~110 dB)
- **Reusability**: Body 20-50, fuze 20-50
- **Initial Cost**: $15-25
- **Per-throw Cost**: $0.50 (partial body replacement)
- **Indigenous**: 85% (PLA molding local)

**Timing**: RÚT (instant) → GIỮ (until throw) → NÉM (instant) → NÚP (immediate) → NỔ (3-5 sec after throw)

```
STOCKS:
- Consumable Inventory (blank cartridges, pyrotechnics)
- Ready Grenades (loaded, ready to use)
- Trained Soldiers (competent grenade throwers)

FLOWS:
- Procurement (consumables entering system)
- Throwing (grenades being used)
- Recovery (grenades being collected)
- Reset (grenades being reloaded)
- Learning (soldiers gaining competence)
```

## 9.3 Feedback Loop Analysis

### Reinforcing Loops (R)

**R1: Training Volume Loop**
```
More Grenades Available → More Training Sessions → More Soldiers Trained 
    → Higher Demand → More Grenades Procured → More Grenades Available
```
- **Implication**: Initial investment in reusable grenades enables virtuous cycle of increased training

**R2: Cost Efficiency Loop**
```
Lower Cost per Throw → More Throws per Budget → More Training → 
    Better Soldiers → Higher Budget Justification → More Procurement → 
    Economies of Scale → Lower Cost per Throw
```
- **Implication**: High-reuse designs (steel body, cheap blanks) enable this loop

**R3: Indigenous Capability Loop**
```
Local Production → Experience → Quality Improvement → 
    Customer Acceptance → More Orders → Scale → Local Production Expansion
```
- **Implication**: Starting with simple design (V4 Low-Cost) builds capability for advanced designs

### Balancing Loops (B)

**B1: Reusability Limit Loop**
```
More Throws → More Wear → Degradation → Replacement Needed → 
    Cost Increase → Fewer Throws (per budget)
```
- **Mitigation**: Design for 500+ cycles, quality materials, maintenance procedures

**B2: Safety Constraint Loop**
```
Louder Sound Desired → Larger Pyrotechnic → More Hazard → 
    Safety Restrictions → Smaller Pyrotechnic → Less Loud Sound
```
- **Mitigation**: Balance sound level (140 dB) against safety (ear protection required, no fragmentation)

**B3: Fidelity-Safety Trade-off**
```
More Realistic → More Hazardous → More Restrictions → 
    Less Accessible Training → Less Realistic Experience
```
- **Mitigation**: Achieve fidelity through handling (weight, delay) not hazard (no fragmentation)

## 9.4 Leverage Point Analysis (Donella Meadows Framework)

| Leverage Point | Application to Training Grenade | Impact |
|----------------|--------------------------------|--------|
| **L2: Goal of the System** | Shift from "simulate explosion" to "build muscle memory and confidence" | Enables non-pyrotechnic solutions (electronic) |
| **L3: Self-Organization** | Enable units to maintain/reset grenades themselves | Reduces logistics dependency, increases training frequency |
| **L4: Rules of the System** | Change range restrictions based on grenade type certification | Opens more training locations |
| **L5: Information Flows** | Add proximity sensors for automatic scoring feedback | Removes subjective adjudication, enables objective assessment |
| **L6: Positive Feedback Loops** | Strengthen R2 (cost efficiency) with indigenous production | Virtuous cycle of capability and cost reduction |
| **L7: Negative Feedback Loops** | Improve B1 (reusability) with better materials | Extend lifecycle, reduce replacement cost |
| **L8: Delays** | Reduce procurement lead time with local production | Faster response to training needs |
| **L9: System Structure** | Modular design (body + fuze + consumable as separate) | Enables component-level optimization |
| **L10: Parameters** | Adjust weight tolerance from ±10% to ±5% | Improves handling fidelity |

**Highest Leverage Intervention**: **L2 Goal Shift**
- From "replicate explosion effects" to "build safe, confident grenade handlers"
- Unlocks willingness to accept electronic (non-pyrotechnic) solutions
- Enables training in more locations (no pyrotechnic restrictions)
- Aligns with future STE-LTS integration (electronic scoring)

## 9.5 Archetype Identification

**Archetype: "Limits to Growth"**
```
Engine: R1 Training Volume Loop
Brake: B1 Reusability Limit Loop

Training volume grows as grenades become available, but eventually 
limited by wear/replacement costs. Solution: Design for maximum reusability 
(500+ cycles) and minimize consumable costs (<$0.10/throw).
```

**Archetype: "Shifting the Burden"**
```
Symptom Fix: Import expensive commercial grenades (quick, high quality)
Fundamental Solution: Develop indigenous capability (slow, builds self-sufficiency)

Risk: Continued import dependence erodes local capability. 
Solution: Phase approach - start indigenous development immediately, 
even if quality is initially lower.
```

# SKILL 12: TARGETED DRILL MASTER - Practice Problems

## 12.1 Drill Set A: Requirements Analysis

### Drill A.1: Weight Tolerance Specification
**Problem**: A training grenade must match M67 weight (400g). What tolerance should be specified, and why?

**Model Answer**:
- **Specification**: D: 400g ±20g (5% tolerance)
- **Rationale**: 
  - ±5% is industry standard for handling fidelity
  - Throwing technique develops muscle memory for specific weight
  - >10% variation would noticeably affect throwing range
  - <2% tolerance is excessive precision, increases cost without training benefit
- **Verification**: Weighing with calibrated scale (±1g precision), sample n≥10

### Drill A.2: Sound Level Requirement
**Problem**: What sound level should be specified for a training grenade, and what PPE is required?

**Model Answer**:
- **Specification**: 
  - D: ≥130 dB at 5m (minimum for realistic feedback)
  - W: 140-167 dB at 1m (stress inoculation effect)
- **PPE Required**:
  - Ear protection mandatory (earplugs ≥NRR 22 or earmuffs ≥NRR 25)
  - Eye protection recommended (safety glasses)
- **Rationale**:
  - <120 dB: Insufficient for stress inoculation (feels like firecracker)
  - >170 dB: Risk of hearing damage even with protection
  - Real M67: ~170 dB at 5m from blast, but with lethal fragmentation
- **Reference**: OSHA permissible exposure: 140 dB peak, single impulse

### Drill A.3: D/W Classification Exercise
**Problem**: Classify the following requirements as Demand (D) or Wish (W):

1. Weight matches target grenade within 5%
2. Sound level ≥167 dB at 1m
3. Zero fragmentation
4. Body survives 1000 throws
5. Cost per throw <$0.10
6. Compatible with MILES system
7. Delay adjustable 2-6 seconds
8. Biodegradable materials

**Model Answer**:
1. **D** (Weight): Critical for handling fidelity, non-negotiable
2. **W** (Sound): 167 dB is ideal but 130 dB acceptable for basic training
3. **D** (Fragmentation): Safety absolute requirement
4. **W** (1000 throws): 500 throws acceptable minimum
5. **W** (Cost): Target, but $0.50 acceptable
6. **W** (MILES): Nice-to-have, not essential for basic training
7. **W** (Adjustable delay): Fixed 4 sec acceptable for standard training
8. **W** (Biodegradable): Environmental benefit, not essential function

## 12.2 Drill Set B: Function Structure

### Drill B.1: Overall Function Statement
**Problem**: Write a solution-neutral overall function statement for training grenade.

**Model Answer**:
- **Poor** (solution-specific): "Detonate blank cartridge after delay to simulate grenade explosion"
- **Good** (solution-neutral): "Simulate live grenade handling and detonation characteristics"
- **Best** (fully abstracted): "Provide realistic grenade handling experience with sensory feedback and absolute safety"

**Why "Best" is preferred**:
- Does not specify "detonation" (could be electronic)
- Does not specify "live grenade" (could match any reference)
- Includes safety as functional requirement (design constraint elevated)
- Enables broader solution space (pyrotechnic, electronic, mechanical)

### Drill B.2: Sub-function Decomposition
**Problem**: Decompose F2 "Arm upon release" to Level 2 sub-functions.

**Model Answer**:
```
F2: ARM UPON RELEASE
├── F2.1: Hold striker under tension
│   └── Spring compressed by lever, 20N force
├── F2.2: Retain lever until throw
│   └── Safety pin prevents lever release
├── F2.3: Release striker when lever departs
│   └── Lever flies off, spring drives striker
├── F2.4: Initiate delay sequence
│   └── Striker impacts primer, starts delay
└── F2.5: Prevent accidental arming
    └── Pin must be deliberately removed, drop-safe
```

**E-M-S for F2**:
- Primary Flow: ENERGY (spring potential → kinetic → chemical)
- Secondary Flow: SIGNAL (mechanical state change: armed/unarmed)

### Drill B.3: E-M-S Classification
**Problem**: Classify the training grenade system as APPARATUS, MACHINE, or PLANT. Justify.

**Model Answer**:
- **Classification**: APPARATUS
- **Justification**:
  - Primary function involves MATERIAL transformation (consumable → noise/smoke)
  - Energy conversion is supporting function, not continuous operation
  - Unlike MACHINE (continuous energy conversion, e.g., motor)
  - Unlike PLANT (continuous material production, e.g., chemical factory)
- **Flow Analysis**:
  - Input: Consumable (blank cartridge) + Energy (spring)
  - Process: Chemical reaction (deflagration)
  - Output: Acoustic energy (sound) + Thermal energy (flash) + Material (smoke)

## 12.5 Drill Set E: Embodiment Design

### Drill E.1: Thread Specification
**Problem**: Specify the fuze-to-body thread for Vietnamese training grenade (RGD-5 profile).

**Model Answer**:
- **Thread**: M14×1.5 (metric, 14mm diameter, 1.5mm pitch)
- **Rationale**:
  - RGD-5 uses UZRGM fuze with approximately M14 thread
  - Metric standard available in Vietnam (ISO)
  - 1.5mm pitch provides secure engagement without excessive turns
  - Thread length: 10mm minimum for 6+ threads engagement
- **Tolerance**: 6H/6g (medium fit, allows field assembly)
- **Material**: Body = steel (female thread), Fuze = aluminum or steel (male thread)
- **Locking**: Thread sealant or locking wire provision

### Drill E.2: Drop Test Specification
**Problem**: Specify drop test requirements for training grenade body. Reference MIL-STD-810H.

**Model Answer**:
- **Test Method**: MIL-STD-810H Method 516.8 (Shock)
- **Test Procedure**:
  1. Drop grenade from 2m height
  2. Impact surface: Concrete (shore hardness ≥85)
  3. Orientation: 6 orientations (±X, ±Y, ±Z axes)
  4. Repetitions: 5 drops per orientation = 30 total
  5. Condition: Room temperature (23°C ±5°C)
- **Pass Criteria**:
  - No visible cracks or fractures
  - No sharp edges created
  - Fuze thread functional (accepts fuze)
  - Weight within tolerance after test
  - Mechanism functions normally (striker, lever)
- **Documentation**: Photo before/after, dimensional check, function test

|------|--------|------|------|

# APPENDIX A: KEY FORMULAS AND CALCULATIONS

## A.1 Cost Analysis Formulas

**Lifecycle Cost**:
```
LCC = C_initial + (N_throws × C_throw) + C_maintenance + C_disposal

Where:
- C_initial = Body cost + Fuze mechanism cost
- N_throws = Total throws over lifecycle
- C_throw = Consumable cost per throw
- C_maintenance = Periodic overhaul cost
- C_disposal = End-of-life disposal cost
```

**Cost per Throw (Amortized)**:
```
C_amortized = (C_initial + N_throws × C_consumable) / N_throws
            = C_initial/N_throws + C_consumable

As N_throws → ∞, C_amortized → C_consumable
```

**Example Calculation**:
- Steel body: $50
- Fuze mechanism: $30
- Blank cartridge: $0.10/throw
- Body life: 500 throws
- Fuze life: 200 throws

For 1000 throws:
- Bodies needed: 1000/500 = 2 → $100
- Fuzes needed: 1000/200 = 5 → $150
- Consumables: 1000 × $0.10 = $100
- Total: $100 + $150 + $100 = $350
- Cost per throw: $350/1000 = $0.35

## A.2 Sound Level Calculations

**Sound Pressure Level (SPL)**:
```
SPL = 20 × log₁₀(P/P_ref) [dB]

Where:
- P = Sound pressure [Pa]
- P_ref = Reference pressure = 20 μPa (threshold of hearing)
```

**Distance Attenuation** (spherical spreading):
```
SPL₂ = SPL₁ - 20 × log₁₀(r₂/r₁) [dB]

Where:
- SPL₁ = SPL at distance r₁
- SPL₂ = SPL at distance r₂
```

**Example**: If SPL = 167 dB at 0.1m, what is SPL at 5m?
```
SPL₅ₘ = 167 - 20 × log₁₀(5/0.1)
      = 167 - 20 × log₁₀(50)
      = 167 - 20 × 1.7
      = 167 - 34
      = 133 dB
```

## A.3 Weight and Balance

**Center of Gravity Position**:
```
x_CG = Σ(mᵢ × xᵢ) / Σmᵢ

Where:
- mᵢ = mass of component i
- xᵢ = position of component i center
```

**Moment of Inertia** (for throwing dynamics):
```
I = Σmᵢrᵢ² (about CG)

Where:
- rᵢ = distance from component i to CG
```

**Example**: Training grenade with steel body
- Body shell: 350g, CG at geometric center
- Fuze: 40g, offset 15mm from center
- Ballast: 10g, opposite side, 10mm from center

```
x_CG = (350×0 + 40×15 + 10×(-10)) / (350+40+10)
     = (0 + 600 - 100) / 400
     = 500/400 = 1.25mm from geometric center
```

Acceptable if <5mm offset (specification).

## A.4 Delay Time

**Pyrotechnic Delay**:
```
t_delay = L / v_burn

Where:
- L = Length of delay element [mm]
- v_burn = Burn rate of delay composition [mm/s]
```

**Example**: For 4-second delay with burn rate 3 mm/s:
```
L = t_delay × v_burn = 4s × 3mm/s = 12mm
```

**Tolerance**: Burn rate varies ±10%, so delay = 4s ±0.4s
