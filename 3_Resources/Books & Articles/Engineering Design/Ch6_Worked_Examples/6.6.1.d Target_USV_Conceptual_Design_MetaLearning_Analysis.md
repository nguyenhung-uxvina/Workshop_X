## 1. Executive Summary

### 1.1 Project Context

The Target USV (Unmanned Surface Vehicle for target simulation - Xuồng Không Người Lái Mục Tiêu) is a high-speed autonomous vessel designed to simulate enemy surface threats for naval gunnery and anti-ship missile training. Unlike traditional towed targets, Target USVs provide dynamic, maneuvering threats that realistically challenge shipboard weapon systems and crew decision-making.

**Key Parallel to Section 6.6 Examples**:

| Aspect | Mixing Tap | UAV Catapult | Tethered Drone | Training Grenade | **Target USV** |
|--------|------------|--------------|----------------|------------------|----------------|
| **Main Flow** | Material (water) | Energy (stored) | Energy + Signal | Energy (delayed) | **Energy (propulsion) + Signal (signature)** |
| **Independence** | V̇ ↔ ϑ | v ↔ θ | P ↔ h | R ↔ S | **v (speed) ↔ σ (signature)** |
| **Domain** | Plumbing | Mechanical | Electrical | Pyrotechnic | **Marine + RF/IR** |
| **Unique Challenge** | Pressure coupling | Gravity on rail | Tether weight | Arming sequence | **Sea state + Multi-sensor** |
| **Environment** | Indoor | Outdoor static | Outdoor tethered | Thrown | **Open sea, harsh** |

### 1.2 Design Objectives Summary

| Objective | Specification | Priority |
|-----------|---------------|----------|
| Maximum speed | ≥35 knots | **DEMAND** |
| Cruise endurance | ≥4 hours at 20 knots | **DEMAND** |
| Control range | ≥20 km | **DEMAND** |
| Radar cross-section | 5-50 m² (adjustable) | **DEMAND** |
| Sea state capability | SS0-4 (waves ≤2.5m) | **DEMAND** |
| Safety systems | Failsafe stop, geofence, collision avoidance | **DEMAND** |
| Recovery | Survivable for reuse after near-miss | **DEMAND** |
| Swarm capability | ≥4 coordinated vessels | **WISH** |
| IR signature | Thermal augmentation optional | **WISH** |
| Indigenous content | >60% local manufacturing | **WISH** |
| Unit cost | <$50,000 USD | **WISH** |

### 1.3 Key Results Preview

Following the complete 8-step process:
- **Function Structure Selected**: Independent propulsion, navigation, and signature systems with unified control
- **Working Principle Groups Selected**: Waterjet propulsion + GPS/INS navigation + Passive RCS enhancement
- **Winning Solution Variant**: "Catamaran Hull + Twin Waterjet + Modular Signature" (Variant B)
- **VDI 2225 Score**: 0.82 (highest among 4 variants)
- **Next Steps**: Hull hydrodynamics optimization and indigenous autopilot development

### 3.3 Physical Relationships

**Hydrodynamic Analysis**:
```
Propulsion Power Requirement:
P = R × v
where R = total resistance, v = velocity

For planing hull at 35 knots (18 m/s):
R ≈ ½ρv²C_D A_wet + W/L_D (wave-making)

Typical 500 kg displacement, 5m hull:
R ≈ 1500-2000 N at 35 knots
P = 2000 N × 18 m/s = 36 kW (48 HP) minimum
With 70% efficiency: P_engine ≈ 50-60 kW (67-80 HP)

Fuel consumption at cruise (20 knots):
Specific fuel consumption: ~300 g/kWh
Power at 20 knots: ~15-20 kW
Fuel rate: 6-8 L/hour
4-hour endurance: 30 L minimum, specify 80 L for reserve
```

**Radar Cross-Section Analysis**:
```
Corner Reflector RCS:
σ = (4πa⁴)/(3λ²) for triangular trihedral
where a = edge length, λ = wavelength

For X-band radar (λ = 3 cm) and a = 0.3m:
σ = 4π(0.3)⁴/(3×0.03²) = ~10 m²

For a = 0.5m:
σ = 4π(0.5)⁴/(3×0.03²) = ~75 m²

Target: 5-50 m² adjustable
Solution: Multiple reflector sizes, switchable mounting
```

### 3.4 Independence Requirements

*Paralleling the mixing tap's V̇-ϑ independence*

**Critical Insight**: The Target USV requires:

| Variable | Symbol | Requirement |
|----------|--------|-------------|
| Speed | v | 0-35+ knots, continuously variable |
| Course | φ | Any heading, precise control |
| Radar signature | σ_R | 5-50 m², selectable |
| IR signature | σ_IR | Optional augmentation |

**The v-σ Independence Problem**:

1. **Higher Speed** typically means:
   - More engine heat (larger IR signature)
   - More wake (visual/radar clutter)
   - Different hull attitude (RCS changes with trim)
   
2. **Different Signature** typically requires:
   - Different reflector configurations
   - Heat sources (for IR)
   - Possibly different hull profile

3. **Design Challenge**: Achieve high speed WITHOUT uncontrollable signature changes

**Independence Solution**: Decouple propulsion and signature through:
- Hull design: Planing attitude consistent across speed range
- Propulsion: Enclosed waterjet (minimal wake, hidden from radar)
- Signature: Modular pods independent of propulsion
- Control: Separate throttle and signature commands

### 3.5 Alternative System Concepts Considered

| Alternative | Consideration | Decision |
|-------------|---------------|----------|
| Towed target barge | Simple, cheap | **Dismissed**: No maneuverability |
| Jet ski conversion | Fast, available | **Dismissed**: Limited payload, no autonomy |
| Electric USV | Quiet, green | **Dismissed**: Insufficient endurance |
| Hydrofoil USV | Very fast | **Dismissed**: Complex, expensive |
| **Conventional planing hull** | Proven, manufacturable | **Selected**: Best balance |

**Justified Selection**:
> Conventional planing hull with waterjet propulsion selected because it provides proven high-speed capability, is manufacturable by Vietnamese boat builders, and allows modular signature systems.

## 4. Step 3: Establishing Function Structures

### 4.1 Subfunction Identification

The overall function decomposes into:
- **Propel vessel**: Convert fuel energy to forward motion
- **Navigate**: Determine position and follow commanded path
- **Control**: Receive and execute operator commands
- **Present signatures**: Return detectable signals to training sensors
- **Ensure safety**: Prevent loss of vessel, protect shipping
- **Report status**: Transmit telemetry to control station
- **Survive environment**: Withstand sea state, recover from engagement

**Decision**: Function Structure Variant 3 selected because:

```
Advantage: Superior stability, larger deck for equipment
```

**Decision**: Catamaran recommended for primary variant due to stability advantages; monohull as lower-cost backup.

### 6.2 Working Principle Analysis for Propulsion

**Twin Outboard (Group G)**:
```
Configuration: 2× 40 HP outboard motors (Chinese/Yamaha)
Thrust: ~2400 N total at full throttle
Efficiency: ~45-50% propulsive
Steering: Differential thrust or motor pivot
Maintenance: Easy replacement, widely available parts
Cost: ~$6,000 for pair (Chinese), ~$15,000 (Yamaha)
Advantage: COTS, easy replacement, proven reliability
Disadvantage: Exposed to radar, spray, impact
```

**Single Waterjet (Group F)**:
```
Configuration: Axial flow waterjet, 60 HP diesel engine
Thrust: ~2000 N at 35 knots
Efficiency: ~55-60% at high speed (better than prop at >25 knots)
Steering: Deflector bucket, reverse capability
Maintenance: More complex but protected inside hull
Cost: ~$8,000 (jet unit) + $6,000 (diesel) = $14,000
Advantage: No exposed moving parts, excellent maneuvering
Disadvantage: Higher initial cost, specialized maintenance
```

**Decision**: Waterjet for primary variant (better protection, maneuverability); twin outboard for budget variant.

### 6.3 Working Principle Analysis for Signature

**Passive Corner Reflectors (Group L)**:
```
Configuration: 4× trihedral reflectors, 0.3-0.5m edge
RCS: 10-75 m² per reflector (aspect-dependent)
Total RCS: 5-50 m² adjustable by number deployed
Mounting: Quick-release poles, 2m above deck
Cost: ~$500 total (aluminum sheet fabrication)
Advantage: Simple, reliable, no power required
Disadvantage: Fixed RCS per configuration
```

**Active RF-SAS (Group M)**:
```
Configuration: Radar signal amplifier, programmable
RCS: 1-500 m² continuously adjustable
Power: 50-100W
Cost: ~$20,000-50,000 (import)
Advantage: Flexible, EW training capability
Disadvantage: Expensive, complex, import-dependent
```

**Decision**: Passive corner reflectors for initial system; RF-SAS as future upgrade option.

## 10. Skill 1: Feynman Technique Analysis

### 10.1 Concept: "Multi-Domain Target Simulation"

#### 60-Second Explanation (ELI12)

A Target USV is like a radio-controlled boat on steroids that plays "pretend enemy ship" for the navy to practice shooting at!

Here's what makes it special:
1. **It goes FAST** - 35+ knots (like a car at 65 km/h on water)
2. **It zigzags** - Like a video game character dodging bullets
3. **It looks BIG on radar** - Metal reflectors make it seem like a much bigger boat
4. **It's SMART** - GPS and computer brain let it follow routes automatically
5. **It's SAFE** - If it loses radio contact, it stops and waits to be rescued

The cool part: The navy can practice with REAL guns and missiles against something that moves like a REAL enemy, but nobody gets hurt and they can use it again tomorrow!

#### Everyday Analogy

Think of a Target USV like a **drone for the water**:
- **Drone body** = Boat hull (designed to go fast)
- **Drone motors** = Waterjet (pushes water out the back)
- **Drone GPS** = Same GPS, tells it where it is
- **Drone radio** = Same radio, but works over 20 km
- **BUT ALSO**: Metal umbrellas (corner reflectors) that reflect radar

The difference from a drone: You can't just crash it into things - it costs $45,000!

### 10.2 Concept: "v-σ Independence"

#### 60-Second Explanation

The tricky part of designing a Target USV: How do you control SPEED and RADAR SIGNATURE separately?

**The Problem**:
- When a boat goes faster, the engine gets hotter (bigger IR signature)
- When a boat goes faster, the wake gets bigger (visible on radar)
- The boat tilts differently at different speeds (changes radar return)

**The Solution**:
1. Hide the engine INSIDE the hull (no visible heat)
2. Use a waterjet (no exposed propeller, minimal wake)
3. Put radar reflectors on POLES above the hull (independent of boat angle)
4. Control reflector size separately from throttle

It's like making a race car where you can change how bright the headlights are without changing how fast you drive!

## 21. Skill 12: Targeted Drill Exercises

### 21.1 Drill Set A: Power Calculations

**Drill A1**: Calculate required power for 500 kg USV at 35 knots.

_Model Answer_:
```
Speed: v = 35 knots = 18 m/s
Displacement: m = 500 kg
Estimated resistance coefficient: C_R ≈ 0.004 (planing hull)
Wetted area: A_wet ≈ 4 m²

Drag force:
R = ½ρv²C_R A_wet = ½ × 1025 × 18² × 0.004 × 4
R ≈ 2,660 N

Power at propeller:
P = R × v = 2,660 × 18 = 47,880 W ≈ 48 kW

With 70% propulsive efficiency:
P_engine = 48 / 0.70 = 68.5 kW ≈ 92 HP

Select: 60-80 HP engine with margin ✓
```

**Drill A2**: Calculate fuel consumption for 4-hour cruise at 20 knots.

_Model Answer_:
```
Power at 20 knots (v = 10.3 m/s):
P ≈ 15 kW (scaled from Drill A1, P ∝ v³)

Specific fuel consumption: SFC = 300 g/kWh
Fuel rate: 15 kW × 0.3 kg/kWh = 4.5 kg/hr ≈ 5.6 L/hr

4-hour cruise: 5.6 × 4 = 22.4 L
With 30% reserve: 22.4 / 0.70 = 32 L minimum
Specify: 80 L tank for margin ✓
```

### 21.2 Drill Set B: RCS Calculations

**Drill B1**: Size corner reflector for 20 m² RCS at X-band (λ = 3 cm).

_Model Answer_:
```
Trihedral corner reflector RCS formula:
σ = (4πa⁴)/(3λ²)

For σ = 20 m², λ = 0.03 m:
20 = (4π × a⁴)/(3 × 0.03²)
a⁴ = 20 × 3 × 0.0009 / (4π)
a⁴ = 0.00429
a = 0.256 m ≈ 26 cm edge length

Practical: Use 2× reflectors with a = 20 cm each (~8 m² each)
Or 1× reflector with a = 30 cm (~30 m²)
```

**Drill B2**: How many 0.3m reflectors needed for 50 m² total?

_Model Answer_:
```
Single 0.3m reflector:
σ_1 = (4π × 0.3⁴)/(3 × 0.03²) = (4π × 0.0081)/(0.0027) = 37.7 m²

For 50 m² total:
N = 50 / 37.7 = 1.33 → 2 reflectors

But RCS not simply additive (depends on aspect angle)
Conservative: Use 2× 0.3m reflectors positioned at different angles
Achieves 30-75 m² depending on aspect ✓
```

### 21.3 Drill Set C: Endurance Analysis

**Drill C1**: Calculate maximum range at 20 knots with 80L fuel.

_Model Answer_:
```
Fuel: 80 L
Consumption at 20 knots: 5.6 L/hr (from Drill A2)
Endurance: 80 / 5.6 = 14.3 hours

BUT: Requirement is 4 hours with 30% reserve
Actual available fuel: 80 × 0.70 = 56 L
Usable endurance: 56 / 5.6 = 10 hours ✓

Range at 20 knots:
Speed: 20 knots × 1.852 km/hr = 37 km/hr
Range: 10 hours × 37 km/hr = 370 km ✓
```
