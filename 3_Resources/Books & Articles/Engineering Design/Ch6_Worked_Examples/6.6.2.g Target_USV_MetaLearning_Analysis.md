## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

### Core Design Paradigm: AUTONOMY-CENTERED

**Target USV is fundamentally AUTONOMY-CENTERED** (unlike Towed Target=tow system-centered, Target UAV=signature-centered). The core principle: **Autonomous navigation and survivability determine operational capability, training realism, and lifecycle economics**.

**Critical Systems Hierarchy:**
```
AUTONOMY (Control + Navigation) → Determines operational envelope
    ↓
PROPULSION (Speed + Endurance) → Determines training scenario scope
    ↓
SIGNATURE (Radar + IR + Visual) → Determines threat simulation fidelity
    ↓
SURVIVABILITY (Damage tolerance + Recovery) → Determines lifecycle cost
```

**Essential Problem Statement (Solution-Neutral):**
> "PROVIDE REALISTIC MANEUVERING SURFACE THREAT PRESENTATION FOR NAVAL WEAPONS TRAINING WHILE ENSURING OPERATIONAL SAFETY, MAXIMIZING REUSABILITY, AND MINIMIZING PER-ENGAGEMENT COST AT AFFORDABLE INDIGENOUS CAPABILITY LEVEL"

### Key Differentiators from Other Target Systems

| Aspect | Target USV | Towed Target | Target UAV |
|--------|------------|--------------|------------|
| **Primary Driver** | Autonomy/Control | Cable Mechanics | Signature Fidelity |
| **Maneuverability** | HIGH (independent motion) | LOW (tow line constrained) | MEDIUM (flight dynamics) |
| **Reusability** | HIGH (50-200+ uses) | MEDIUM (5-20 uses) | LOW-MEDIUM (1-10 uses) |
| **Initial Cost** | HIGH ($50K-$500K) | LOW ($5K-$30K) | MEDIUM ($30K-$500K) |
| **Per-Engagement Cost** | LOW ($500-$2,000) | MEDIUM ($1,500-$3,000) | HIGH ($5K-$50K) |
| **Indigenous Potential** | 65-85% | 85-95% | 55-75% |
| **Complexity** | HIGH | LOW | VERY HIGH |

## SKILL 1: FEYNMAN TECHNIQUE - EXPLAIN SIMPLY

### 1.1 ELI5 (Explain Like I'm 5)

**"Target USV is like a robot boat that plays hide-and-seek with big ships. The sailors on the ship practice shooting at the robot boat. The robot boat is very smart - it can drive itself, turn quickly, and try to dodge. But it's designed so even if it gets hit, it can often keep floating and be fixed later. This way, sailors can practice shooting at moving boats without hurting anyone."**

### 1.2 ELI12 (Explain Like I'm 12)

**"Target USV is an unmanned boat controlled by remote or computer. Naval forces use it to practice firing their guns and missiles at a moving target on water. Unlike a simple target being pulled behind another boat, this robot boat can maneuver on its own - zigzagging, speeding up, slowing down - just like an enemy attack craft would. It has special equipment to make it show up on radar and thermal cameras. If hit by small caliber rounds, it's designed to survive and be repaired for next training session. This makes training realistic without risking manned vessels."**

### 1.3 Everyday Analogy

**Analogy: TARGET USV is like a ADVANCED DRONE CAR FOR DRIVING SCHOOL PRACTICE**

| Drone Car Feature | Target USV Equivalent |
|-------------------|----------------------|
| Remote control + autopilot | GCS + waypoint navigation |
| Crash-resistant bumpers | Foam-filled compartments, damage-tolerant hull |
| GPS tracking for instructor | Telemetry link for range control |
| Can be programmed for different scenarios | Programmable attack patterns (zig-zag, swarm, straight-on) |
| Survives minor collisions | Survives small caliber hits |
| Expensive initial investment, cheap per-session | $50K+ initial, $500-2000 per training mission |
| Better than using real cars to crash | Better than using real enemy boats or missiles |

### 1.4 Core Concept Simplification

**The 5 Essential Questions for Target USV:**

1. **WHAT does it DO?** → Simulates enemy surface vessel for weapons training
2. **HOW does it MOVE?** → Self-propelled with engine/motor (not towed)
3. **HOW is it CONTROLLED?** → Remote control + autonomous waypoint navigation
4. **HOW does it LOOK to sensors?** → Enhanced radar (corner reflectors) + IR (heaters) + visual (profile)
5. **WHAT happens when HIT?** → Survives small caliber, may sink on large caliber (expendable option available)

#### B.2 Overall Function & Black-Box

**Overall Function Statement:**
> "Convert control commands + fuel/electrical energy into realistic maneuvering surface target presentation with configurable radar/IR/visual signatures for naval weapons training"

**Black-Box Representation:**

```
INPUTS:                           OUTPUTS:
─────────────────                 ─────────────────
Radio commands ────────┐    ┌──── Position/motion (target for tracking)
GPS signals ───────────┤    ├──── Radar signature (RCS for radar lock)
Fuel/electricity ──────┤ ■  ├──── IR signature (for FLIR training)
Environmental data ────┤    ├──── Visual presence (day/night visible)
Scenario parameters ───┘    └──── Telemetry data (status to GCS)
                        
         ■ = TARGET USV SYSTEM
```

**Transformation:** Control information + stored energy → Motion + signatures + status

**Corner Reflector RCS Formula:**
```
σ = (4π × a⁴) / (3 × λ²)

Where:
σ = Radar Cross Section (m²)
a = Edge length of trihedral (m)
λ = Radar wavelength (m)

Example: X-band (λ = 0.03m), a = 0.3m
σ = (4π × 0.0081) / (3 × 0.0009) = 37.7 m²
```

**BMT-01/HN Radar Specification:**

| **Electric hot plate** | +50-150°C | 500-2000W | LWIR | 85% |
| **Flare** | +500-1000°C | N/A | All bands | 30% |

**IR Physics:**
```
Stefan-Boltzmann Law:
P = ε × σ × A × T⁴

Where:
P = Radiated power (W)
ε = Emissivity (0.9 for black body approximation)
σ = Stefan-Boltzmann constant (5.67×10⁻⁸ W/m²K⁴)
A = Emitting area (m²)
T = Absolute temperature (K)

Example: 0.5m² panel at 80°C above ambient (30°C → 110°C = 383K)
P = 0.9 × 5.67×10⁻⁸ × 0.5 × 383⁴ = 548 W emitted
```

**BMT-01/HN IR Specification:**

### 12.3 Drill Set C: Signature Calculations

**Drill C.1: Corner Reflector RCS**
> Calculate the RCS of a trihedral corner reflector with 0.4m edge length at X-band (10 GHz, λ=0.03m). Does it meet the requirement for 50 m² RCS?

**Model Answer:**
```
Given:
- Edge length a = 0.4 m
- Wavelength λ = 0.03 m (X-band)

Formula:
σ = (4π × a⁴) / (3 × λ²)

Calculation:
σ = (4 × 3.14159 × 0.4⁴) / (3 × 0.03²)
σ = (12.566 × 0.0256) / (3 × 0.0009)
σ = 0.322 / 0.0027
σ = 119.3 m²

Answer: YES, 119.3 m² exceeds 50 m² requirement
With this reflector, RCS margin is 119.3/50 = 2.4× (good margin)

For exactly 50 m² need:
a⁴ = (50 × 3 × 0.0009) / (4π) = 0.0107
a = 0.32 m edge length minimum
```

**Drill C.2: IR Power Requirement**
> A Target USV needs to be detected at 5 km by a FLIR camera. The FLIR detects +20°C above ambient at 3 km. Calculate required heater power for 0.5 m² emitter.

**Model Answer:**
```
Detection Physics:
Range ∝ √(ΔT × A)
At 3 km: detects ΔT₁ = 20°C
At 5 km: need ΔT₂

Scaling:
(5/3)² = ΔT₂/ΔT₁
2.78 = ΔT₂/20
ΔT₂ = 55.6°C above ambient

For 0.5 m² at +55.6°C (ambient 30°C → 85.6°C = 358.6K):
P = ε × σ × A × (T⁴ - T_amb⁴)
P = 0.9 × 5.67×10⁻⁸ × 0.5 × (358.6⁴ - 303⁴)
P = 5.1×10⁻⁸ × 0.5 × (1.65×10¹⁰ - 0.84×10¹⁰)
P = 2.55×10⁻⁸ × 0.81×10¹⁰
P = 206 W radiated

Heater power (50% efficiency):
P_heater = 206/0.5 = 412 W minimum
Recommend: 600-800 W for margin

Answer: ~600-800W heater power for 5 km detection
```
