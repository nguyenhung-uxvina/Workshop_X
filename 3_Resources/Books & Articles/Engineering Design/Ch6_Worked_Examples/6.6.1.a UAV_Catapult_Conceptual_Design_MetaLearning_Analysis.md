## 1. Executive Summary

### 1.1 Project Context

The UAV Catapult is a launch system for fixed-wing unmanned aerial vehicles that cannot take off from short runways or confined spaces such as naval vessels, islands, and border outposts. This conceptual design follows the identical 8-step methodology demonstrated in Pahl & Beitz Section 6.6 for the one-handed mixing tap.

**Key Parallel to Mixing Tap Example**:

| Aspect | Mixing Tap | UAV Catapult |
|--------|------------|--------------|
| Main flow type | **Material** (water) | **Energy** (kinetic) |
| Independence requirement | V̇ (flow) and ϑ (temperature) | v (velocity) and θ (angle) |
| Function structure challenge | Meter before/after mixing | Store before/during release |
| Working principle variety | Valve types, seat geometries | Energy storage mechanisms |
| VDI 2225 decision | Solution B (cylinder) | To be determined |

### 1.2 Design Objectives Summary

| Objective | Specification | Priority |
|-----------|---------------|----------|
| Launch UAV 15-50 kg MTOW | Exit velocity 20-30 m/s | **DEMAND** |
| Portable deployment | 2-person setup, <30 minutes | **DEMAND** |
| Field reliability | 1000+ launches without major maintenance | **DEMAND** |
| Power independence | No external generator required | **WISH** |
| Indigenous manufacturing | >60% local content | **WISH** |
| Cost target | <$50,000 USD per unit | **WISH** |

### 1.3 Key Results Preview

Following the complete 8-step process:
- **Function Structure Selected**: Independent energy storage and angle adjustment with combined release
- **Working Principle Group Selected**: Elastic/bungee systems (tangential energy release)
- **Winning Solution Variant**: "Tensioned Bungee with Pneumatic Assist" (Variant B)
- **Next Steps**: Dimensional layout with energy consistency validation

## 3. Step 2: Abstracting to Identify Essential Problems

### 3.1 Problem Formulation

*Following the pattern of Figure 6.28*

From the requirements list, the essential problem can be stated:

> **Convert stored energy into kinetic energy of a UAV, achieving target velocity within constrained acceleration, with independent control of exit velocity and launch angle.**

### 3.3 Physical Relationships

*Following the pattern of Figure 6.29*

**Energy Requirement**:
```
E_kinetic = ½ × m × v²

For m = 50 kg, v = 25 m/s:
E_kinetic = ½ × 50 × 25² = 15,625 J ≈ 15.6 kJ

With efficiency η = 0.70:
E_stored = E_kinetic / η = 15.6 / 0.70 = 22.3 kJ required
```

**Acceleration Constraint**:
```
Using kinematic equation: v² = u² + 2as
Where u = 0 (initial velocity), v = 25 m/s, s = 3.5 m (rail length)

a = v² / (2s) = 625 / 7 = 89.3 m/s² ≈ 9.1g

This is within the 12g limit ✓
```

**Force Requirement**:
```
F = m × a = 50 × 89.3 = 4,465 N ≈ 4.5 kN average

Peak force (assuming non-uniform acceleration) ≈ 6-8 kN
```

**Launch Time**:
```
t = v / a = 25 / 89.3 = 0.28 seconds

Power = E / t = 15,600 / 0.28 = 55.7 kW instantaneous
```

### 3.4 Independence Requirements

*Directly paralleling the mixing tap's V̇-ϑ independence*

**Critical Insight**: Just as the mixing tap required independent control of flow rate (V̇) and temperature (ϑ), the UAV catapult requires:

| Variable | Symbol | Requirement |
|----------|--------|-------------|
| Exit velocity | v | Adjustable 20-30 m/s |
| Launch angle | θ | Adjustable 10-30° |

**Independence Conditions**:

1. **Upon changing velocity setting (S_v)**: 
   - Launch angle θ must remain unchanged
   - Trajectory direction independent of energy setting

2. **Upon changing angle setting (S_θ)**:
   - Exit velocity v must remain unchanged
   - Energy delivery independent of rail orientation

3. **Coupling Effects to Avoid**:
   - Rail angle affecting effective launch force (gravity component)
   - Energy storage orientation affecting energy delivery

### 3.5 Alternative Physical Effects Considered

*Following the justified dismissal pattern*

| Alternative | Consideration | Decision |
|-------------|---------------|----------|
| Rocket-assisted takeoff (RATO) | Provides thrust directly to UAV | **Dismissed**: Consumable, expensive per launch, not reusable |
| Wheeled ground acceleration | Vehicle tows UAV to speed | **Dismissed**: Requires runway, defeats purpose |
| Vertical launch (like missiles) | Different architecture | **Dismissed**: Different UAV type required |
| Aircraft carrier-style cable | Steam/electromagnetic catapult | **Dismissed**: Excessive complexity, ship infrastructure |

**Justified Selection**: 
> Rail-based catapult using stored mechanical/pneumatic energy was selected as the proven approach for tactical UAV launch. This parallels the mixing tap's selection of valve-based flow control as the proven physical principle.

## 4. Step 3: Establishing Function Structures

### 4.1 Subfunction Identification

The overall function can be decomposed into:
- **Store energy**: Accumulate launch energy from input
- **Hold UAV**: Secure UAV until launch command
- **Set velocity**: Adjust energy release for target velocity
- **Set angle**: Orient rail to launch direction
- **Release**: Initiate energy transfer
- **Accelerate**: Transfer energy to UAV along rail
- **Separate**: Clean UAV departure from carriage
- **Decelerate carriage**: Stop carriage safely at rail end
- **Reset**: Return to launch-ready state

### 4.2 Function Structure Variant 1: Sequential Energy-Then-Angle

*Paralleling Figure 6.30 (meter flow before mixing)*

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│           FUNCTION STRUCTURE VARIANT 1: SEQUENTIAL (E → θ)            │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                        │
│  Energy ──►[STORE E]──►[SET v]──►[ADJUST θ]──►[RELEASE]──►[ACCEL]──►UAV│
│  Input                                                          at v,θ│
│                                                                        │
│       ┌──────────┐    ┌──────────┐    ┌──────────┐                    │
│       │  Store   │    │  Energy  │    │  Angle   │                    │
│       │  Energy  │───►│  Adjust  │───►│  Adjust  │───►[Launch]        │
│       │  (fixed) │    │  (S_v)   │    │  (S_θ)   │                    │
│       └──────────┘    └──────────┘    └──────────┘                    │
│                                                                        │
│  BEHAVIOR ANALYSIS:                                                   │
│  • Energy stored at fixed level, then metered for velocity            │
│  • Angle adjustment occurs AFTER energy setting                       │
│  • Problem: Changing angle changes effective gravity component        │
│             At θ=30°, gravity reduces effective acceleration          │
│             Velocity setting becomes angle-dependent                  │
│                                                                        │
│  CHARACTERISTIC:                                                       │
│  v_actual = f(S_v, θ) ← NOT INDEPENDENT                               │
│  At θ=10°: v = 25 m/s                                                 │
│  At θ=30°: v = 23.5 m/s (for same S_v) ← 6% error                    │
│                                                                        │
│  VERDICT: REJECTED - v and θ not independent                          │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 4.5 Function Structure Selection Rationale

| Criterion | Variant 1 | Variant 2 | Variant 3 |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| v-θ independence | ❌ Coupled | ⚠️ Conditional | ✅ Independent |
| Mechanism complexity | Low | Medium | Medium |
| Calibration required | Per angle | Per mechanism | Once |
| Behavior predictability | Poor | Fair | **Excellent** |
| **Selection** | Rejected | Backup | **SELECTED** |

**Decision**: Function Structure Variant 3 selected because it achieves true independence between velocity and angle settings, paralleling the mixing tap's selection of the "meter independently then mix" structure for its linear, robust behavior.

## 6. Step 5: Selecting Working Principles

### 6.1 Working Principle Analysis for Selected Groups

**Elastic Systems - Detailed Analysis**:

For bungee/spring systems, the key relationships are:

```
Energy stored: E = ½kx²  (for linear spring)
             E = ∫F dx   (for non-linear bungee)

Force profile: F = kx (decreasing as carriage moves)

Exit velocity: v = √(2E/m × η)
```

**Key characteristics for v-θ independence**:
- Energy storage mechanism mounted at FIXED orientation (horizontal or inclined mount)
- Rail tilts on pivot; carriage connects via flexible coupling
- Gravity effect on carriage during launch can be compensated by adjusting stored energy

**Pneumatic Systems - Detailed Analysis**:

```
Energy stored: E = pV (for isothermal) or E = pV/γ(γ-1) (for adiabatic)

Force profile: F = pA (constant at regulated pressure)

Exit velocity: v = √(2pAs/m) where s = stroke length
```

**Key characteristics for v-θ independence**:
- Pressure independent of cylinder orientation
- **Ideal constant force** provides uniform acceleration
- Precise velocity control via pressure regulation

### 6.2 Selected Working Principles

Based on analysis, the following working principles are selected for principle solution development:

| Principle | Energy Source | Adjustment Method | v-θ Independence |
|-----------|---------------|-------------------|------------------|
| WP-A | Bungee cords (multiple) | Number of cords + pre-stretch | ✓ via fixed mount |
| WP-B | Bungee + pneumatic assist | Pneumatic pressure adjustment | ✓ via pressure |
| WP-C | Pneumatic cylinder | Pressure regulation | ✓ inherent |
| WP-D | Coil spring with cam | Cam profile selection | ✓ via fixed mount |

## 10. Skill 1: Feynman Technique Analysis

### 10.1 Concept: "Energy Flow Design vs Material Flow Design"

#### 60-Second Explanation (ELI12)

The mixing tap controls WATER (stuff that flows). The catapult controls ENERGY (the ability to make things move). They're designed the same way even though one deals with liquid and one deals with force!

Both need to:
1. Store something (water pressure / stretched rubber bands)
2. Control how much comes out (valve opening / how far back you pull)
3. Aim it (hot-cold mix / launch angle)
4. Release it at the right time (turn handle / press launch button)

The key insight: Whether you're designing for water, energy, or even information, the **structure of control** follows the same patterns.

#### Everyday Analogy

Think of a slingshot vs a water balloon launcher:
- **Slingshot** (energy): Pull back rubber band (store energy), aim (set angle), release
- **Water balloon launcher** (material): Fill with water (store material), aim, release

Both work the same way structurally! The catapult is just a bigger slingshot with engineering controls.

#### Defense Application Insight

This pattern applies across all defense systems:
- **Training Grenade**: Store energy (spring tension) → Control release (safety lever) → Deliver effect (delay → bang)
- **Target USV**: Store energy (fuel) → Control release (throttle) → Direct flow (steering)
- **Towed Target**: Store energy (tow vehicle) → Control release (winch tension) → Maintain position (flight surfaces)

### 10.2 Concept: "v-θ Independence = V̇-ϑ Independence"

#### 60-Second Explanation

In the mixing tap, you wanted to change water temperature WITHOUT changing how much water comes out (and vice versa). In the catapult, you want to change launch velocity WITHOUT changing launch angle (and vice versa).

This is called **control independence** - adjusting one thing shouldn't accidentally mess up another thing.

The solution in both cases: **SEPARATE THE CONTROL SYSTEMS** so they don't interfere with each other.

#### Why This Matters for Defense Systems

| System | Variable 1 | Variable 2 | Independence Challenge |
|--------|-----------|-----------|------------------------|
| Mixing Tap | Flow rate | Temperature | Pressure coupling |
| UAV Catapult | Velocity | Angle | Gravity effect on energy |
| Target USV | Speed | Heading | Propulsion/steering coupling |
| Tethered Drone | Power | Altitude | Cable tension effects |

### 10.3 Understanding Check Questions

**Basic**:
1. What type of flow dominates the UAV catapult design? (Material/Energy/Signal)
2. What are the two independently controllable variables?

**Application**:
3. If you increase the launch angle from 15° to 25°, what should happen to the exit velocity? (Stay same / Increase / Decrease)

**Edge Case**:
4. The mixing tap solution involved metering BEFORE mixing. What's the catapult equivalent? (Storing energy BEFORE/AFTER/DURING angle adjustment)

## 21. Skill 12: Targeted Drill Exercises

### 21.1 Drill Set: Energy Calculation

**Drill 1**: Calculate launch energy for 30 kg UAV at 28 m/s with 75% efficiency.

_Model Answer_:
```
E_kinetic = ½ × 30 × 28² = 11,760 J
E_stored = 11,760 / 0.75 = 15,680 J ≈ 15.7 kJ
```

**Drill 2**: Given 3.0 m rail and 25 m/s exit velocity, what's the average acceleration in g?

_Model Answer_:
```
a = v²/(2s) = 625/6 = 104.2 m/s² = 10.6g ✓ (within 12g limit)
```

### 21.2 Drill Set: Function Structure

**Drill 3**: Draw a function structure where angle is set DURING energy release (different from selected structure). Analyze why this wouldn't work.

_Model Answer_: The coupling between energy release dynamics and changing angle would make the system unpredictable. Gravity component changes mid-launch, causing velocity variation.

### 21.3 Drill Set: VDI 2225

**Drill 4**: If "Indigenous manufacturability" weight increases from 3 to 5, recalculate scores for Variants A and B. Does the ranking change?

_Model Answer_:
```
A: 113 + (4×2) = 121
B: 106 + (3×2) = 112
Ranking unchanged, gap widens for A
```
