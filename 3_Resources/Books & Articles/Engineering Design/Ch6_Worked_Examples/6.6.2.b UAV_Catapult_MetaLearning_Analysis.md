# PART I: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY AND SYSTEM CONTEXT

## 1.1 System Overview

A **UAV Catapult** (also called UAV Launcher) is an energy storage and release system that accelerates an unmanned aerial vehicle from rest to flight speed within a constrained distance. This is essential for fixed-wing UAVs that cannot hover or perform vertical takeoff.

### Direct Connection to Pahl & Beitz Section 6.6.2

The **Impulse-Loading Test Rig** example in Section 6.6.2 provides a direct methodological template for UAV Catapult design:

| Test Rig Characteristic | UAV Catapult Parallel |
|-------------------------|----------------------|
| Store energy in flywheel (15,900 J) | Store energy in compressed air/elastic/EM field |
| Release rapidly (260 J in 0.12 s) | Release rapidly (8-15 kJ in 0.5-1.5 s) |
| Controlled rate of increase | Controlled acceleration profile (≤20g) |
| Reproducible impulse | Consistent launch velocity (±2 m/s) |
| Adjustable magnitude | Adjustable for different UAV masses |

## SKILL 1: FEYNMAN TECHNIQUE EXPLANATION (engineering-feynman)

### 1.1 "Explain Like I'm 5" - What is a UAV Catapult?

**Simple Explanation:**

Imagine you want to throw a paper airplane, but it's too heavy to throw by hand. A catapult is like a giant slingshot that pulls back really far, stores up energy, and then WHOOSH - lets go and sends the airplane flying into the sky!

For a real UAV catapult:
- You pump air into a tank (like blowing up a balloon really tight)
- When you're ready, you press a button
- The air rushes out and pushes the airplane down a track
- The airplane goes from stopped to flying speed in less than a second!

### 1.2 The Core Physics (Simplified)

**Why do UAVs need catapults?**

```
AIRPLANE PHYSICS BASICS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

For an airplane to fly, it needs LIFT.
Lift comes from air moving over the wings.

    LIFT = ½ × ρ × v² × A × Cₗ
    
    Where:
    ρ = air density (can't change)
    v = velocity (WE CONTROL THIS!)
    A = wing area (fixed by design)
    Cₗ = lift coefficient (fixed by design)
    
Minimum flight speed (stall speed):
    For a 25 kg UAV with 1.5 m² wing: v_stall ≈ 15-18 m/s

PROBLEM: How do we get from 0 m/s to 20+ m/s quickly?

SOLUTION 1: Long runway (like big airports)
    → Accelerate slowly over 100-500m
    → NOT possible on ships or small islands!

SOLUTION 2: Catapult (like aircraft carriers)
    → Accelerate quickly over 3-10m
    → Works in tight spaces ✓
```

### 1.3 Energy Storage Methods Explained Simply

**Method 1: Bungee (Like a Rubber Band)**
```
    ←─────────────────────────────────────────→
    
    [WINCH]═══════[ELASTIC CORD]═══════[UAV CART]
    
    1. Winch pulls cart back, stretching cord
    2. Cord stores elastic potential energy
    3. Release! Cord snaps back, pulls cart forward
    4. UAV separates and flies away
    
    Like pulling back a slingshot 🪃
```

**Method 2: Pneumatic (Like an Air Cannon)**
```
    ┌─────────────────┐      ┌─────────────┐
    │  HIGH PRESSURE  │══════│   CYLINDER  │═══════[UAV CART]
    │  AIR TANK       │ VALVE│   (Piston)  │
    │  200-300 bar    │      │             │
    └─────────────────┘      └─────────────┘
    
    1. Compressor fills tank with high-pressure air
    2. Valve opens instantly
    3. Air rushes into cylinder, pushes piston
    4. Piston pushes cart, cart pushes UAV
    
    Like a giant air gun 🎯
```

**Method 3: Electromagnetic (Like a Maglev Train)**
```
    ┌─────┐ ┌─────┐ ┌─────┐ ┌─────┐ ┌─────┐
    │COIL │ │COIL │ │COIL │ │COIL │ │COIL │
    └──┬──┘ └──┬──┘ └──┬──┘ └──┬──┘ └──┬──┘
       │       │       │       │       │
    ═══▓═══════▓═══════▓═══════▓═══════▓═══════
       │       │       │       │       │
       └───────┴───────┴───────┴───────┘
                      │
              [MAGNETIC CART + UAV]
    
    1. Coils energized in sequence (very fast!)
    2. Magnetic field pulls cart forward
    3. No physical contact = no wear
    4. Very smooth acceleration
    
    Like pushing with invisible magnets 🧲
```

### 1.6 Feynman Test Questions

**Q1**: Why do pneumatic catapults provide smoother acceleration than bungee?

**Model Answer**: Pneumatic systems have nearly constant pressure during the stroke (air is compressible but pressure drops gradually), so force stays relatively constant. Bungee force follows Hooke's Law - maximum at full stretch, decreasing as it relaxes. This creates high initial acceleration that drops off, which can stress the UAV airframe. Pneumatic is more like "pushing with constant force" while bungee is "snapping with decreasing force."

**Q2**: What limits the maximum launch velocity of an electromagnetic catapult?

**Model Answer**: Three limits: (1) Power electronics - switching speed of coils limits how fast the magnetic wave can travel; (2) Energy storage - capacitors must store enough energy and discharge fast enough; (3) Track length - longer track means more time to accelerate, but adds weight and cost. For aircraft carriers, EMALS achieves ~100 m/s with ~100m track. For UAVs, 30-50 m/s is typical with 3-6m track.

**Q3**: Why does the test rig flywheel only lose 0.8% of its energy per impulse?

**Model Answer**: The flywheel stores E = ½Jω² = 15,900 J at 1200 rpm. Each impulse only needs 260 J (about 1.6% of stored energy). After releasing 260 J, the flywheel still has 15,640 J, which corresponds to ω = 125 rad/s = 1190 rpm. The small energy extraction means the flywheel acts as a stable energy reservoir, and a small motor can slowly replenish between tests. Same principle applies to pneumatic tanks - a large tank at 200 bar might drop to 190 bar after one launch, easily recharged.

#### D2: Preliminary Calculations - Variant 1 (Pneumatic-Piston)

**Launch Energy:**
```
UAV mass:           m_uav = 25 kg
Carriage mass:      m_car = 8 kg
Total mass:         m_tot = 33 kg
Launch velocity:    v = 25 m/s

Kinetic energy:     E_k = ½ × 33 × 25² = 10,312 J

Add losses (15%):   E_total = 10,312 / 0.85 = 12,132 J

Design target:      E_design = 15,000 J (with margin)
```

**Cylinder Sizing:**
```
Rail length:        L = 4 m
Average acceleration: a = v²/(2L) = 625/8 = 78 m/s² = 8.0g ✓ (<20g)

Average force:      F = m_tot × a = 33 × 78 = 2,574 N
Peak force (1.3×):  F_peak = 3,346 N

Cylinder bore at 10 bar:
    A = F_peak / P = 3346 / (10×10⁵) = 33.5 cm²
    D = √(4A/π) = 6.5 cm → Use Ø80mm cylinder

Verify energy:
    E = F × L = 2574 × 4 = 10,296 J ≈ 10.3 kJ ✓
```

**Tank Sizing:**
```
Cylinder volume:    V_cyl = π/4 × 0.08² × 4 = 0.0201 m³ = 20.1 liters
Working pressure:   P_work = 10 bar
Expansion ratio:    r = 1.4 (polytrophic for fast expansion)

Tank volume at 200 bar (for 5 launches without recharge):
    V_tank = 5 × V_cyl × (P_work/P_tank)^(1/r)
    V_tank = 5 × 20.1 × (10/200)^0.71 = 5 × 20.1 × 0.11 = 11 liters
    
Use 20-liter tank for margin → ~9 launches per fill
```

```
Using kinematics: v² = 2aL
Average acceleration: a_avg = v²/(2L) = 625/8 = 78.1 m/s²
This is 78.1/9.8 = 8.0g ✓ (well under 20g limit)

Average force: F_avg = m × a = 33 × 78.1 = 2,577 N
```

**Step 3: Cylinder Sizing**
```
Working pressure: P = 10 bar = 1,000,000 Pa
Required area: A = F/P = 2577/1,000,000 = 0.00258 m² = 25.8 cm²
Diameter: D = √(4A/π) = √(4×25.8/π) = 5.73 cm

Select standard: Ø80 mm bore (provides margin)
Actual area: A = π×4² = 50.3 cm² = 0.00503 m²
Actual force at 10 bar: F = 5,030 N (good margin)
```

**Step 4: Tank Sizing**
```
Cylinder volume: V_cyl = A × L = 0.00503 × 4 = 0.0201 m³ = 20.1 liters

For isothermal expansion (slow relative to acoustic speed):
P₁V₁ = P₂V₂
Tank at 200 bar, cylinder works at average 10 bar

Per launch: V_tank_used = V_cyl × (P_avg/P_tank) = 20.1 × (10/200) = 1.0 liter

For 10 launches without recharge:
V_tank = 10 × 1.0 = 10 liters minimum
Select: 20-liter tank at 200 bar → 20 launches possible
```

**Step 5: Compressor Sizing**
```
Recharge time target: t = 5 minutes = 300 s
Air to compress: 10 liters at 200 bar = 2000 liters at 1 bar

Compressor flow: Q = 2000/300 = 6.7 liters/s = 400 liters/min
Select: Portable compressor ~500 L/min, 200 bar rated
Power: ~4-5 kW electric
```

#### Bungee System (V2) Complete Sizing

**Given Requirements:**
```
UAV mass:           m_uav = 20 kg (lighter UAV for bungee)
Carriage mass:      m_car = 5 kg
Launch velocity:    v = 22 m/s
Rail length:        L = 4 m
```

**Step 1: Energy Required**
```
Total mass: m_tot = 25 kg
Kinetic energy: E_k = ½ × 25 × 22² = 6,050 J
With 65% efficiency: E_stored = 6050/0.65 = 9,308 J ≈ 9.5 kJ
```

**Step 2: Bungee Selection**
```
Natural rubber bungee characteristics:
- Energy density: ~10-15 kJ/kg (high-quality latex)
- Maximum stretch: 300-400% of relaxed length
- Force at max stretch: varies with cord diameter

For 9.5 kJ with 4 cords:
Energy per cord: 9.5/4 = 2.4 kJ

Cord selection: 20mm diameter latex tube
- Relaxed length: 2.0 m
- Stretched length: 6.0 m (300% stretch)
- Energy stored: ~2.5 kJ per cord ✓

Force at max stretch: F = kx
Spring constant k ≈ 500 N/m (for 20mm tube)
Extension x = 4.0 m
Force per cord: F = 500 × 4 = 2,000 N
Total force: 4 × 2,000 = 8,000 N

Initial acceleration: a = F/m = 8000/25 = 320 m/s² = 32.6g
⚠ EXCEEDS 20g LIMIT!

Mitigation: Use 6 cords with less stretch
- 6 cords × 1.5 kJ each = 9 kJ
- Less stretch = lower initial force
- Or accept higher initial g for simple system
```

## Appendix A: Key Formulas

```
ENERGY FORMULAS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Kinetic Energy:
    E = ½mv²
    
    Example: E = ½ × 25 × 25² = 7,812.5 J

Pneumatic Energy:
    E = P₁V₁ × ln(P₁/P₂)  [isothermal]
    E = P₁V₁/(γ-1) × [1-(P₂/P₁)^((γ-1)/γ)]  [adiabatic]

Elastic Energy:
    E = ½kx²
    
    k = spring constant (N/m)
    x = extension (m)

Acceleration:
    a = v²/(2L)  [constant acceleration]
    
    v = final velocity (m/s)
    L = rail length (m)

Force:
    F = ma
    F = PA  [pneumatic: pressure × area]
```
