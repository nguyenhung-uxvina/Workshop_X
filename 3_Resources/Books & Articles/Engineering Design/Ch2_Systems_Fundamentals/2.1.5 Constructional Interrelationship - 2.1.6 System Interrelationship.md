# PART 2: SKILL 1 - FEYNMAN TECHNIQUE ANALYSIS

## 2.1 Section 2.1.5: Constructional Interrelationship

### 💡 60-SECOND EXPLANATION

The **Construction Structure** is the final physical blueprint of your system. While the Working Structure says HOW the system achieves its function (using physical effects and principles), the Construction Structure says WHAT PARTS actually exist, how they connect, and how they can be produced, assembled, and transported.

Think of it this way: the working structure is like a recipe (combine ingredients using heat, mix, cool), while the construction structure is the actual kitchen setup (these pots, this stove, arranged in this way, delivered by this truck).

### 🏠 EVERYDAY ANALOGY

**Recipe vs. Kitchen Setup:**
- **Working Structure** = Recipe: "Apply heat to water until boiling, add rice, simmer 20 minutes"
- **Construction Structure** = Kitchen: "Use the 2-liter aluminum pot on the right burner, gas stove Model XY, rice stored in upper cabinet drawer #3"

The recipe can work in many kitchens. The kitchen setup IS the concrete implementation.

### 🎯 DEFENSE EXAMPLE: Target USV

**Working Structure Level:**
- "Propel vessel using marine propulsion principle"
- "Control direction using hydrodynamic steering effect"
- "Receive commands using radio signal transmission"

**Construction Structure Level:**
- **Component:** Yamaha 40HP outboard motor (propulsion)
- **Assembly:** Steering bracket with hydraulic cylinder + rudder plate
- **Machine:** Complete Target USV hull (8m fiberglass)
- **Interconnections:** Motor mount bolts M12 × 4, hydraulic lines 1/2", signal cables Cat5e
- **Production consideration:** Hull laid up in mold, motor off-the-shelf
- **Assembly consideration:** Motor can be removed for transport
- **Transport consideration:** Vessel fits 40ft container

### ❓ SELF-CHECK QUESTIONS

1. If someone shows you a CAD drawing with dimensions and materials, are you looking at a Working Structure or Construction Structure?
2. Why does the Construction Structure need to consider "production, assembly, transport"?
3. Can two different Construction Structures satisfy the same Working Structure?

### ❌ COMMON MISCONCEPTION

**Wrong:** "Construction Structure is just the detailed drawings"
**Right:** Construction Structure defines the ENTIRE physical realization including HOW it will be produced, assembled, and transported—not just what it looks like.

## 2.2 Section 2.1.6: System Interrelationship

### 💡 60-SECOND EXPLANATION

**No technical system operates alone.** The System Interrelationship acknowledges that your design exists within a larger context involving:
1. **Humans** who operate and interact with it
2. **Environment** that affects it (and is affected by it)
3. **Other systems** that connect to it

Most importantly, there are FIVE types of effects you must track:
- **Intended Effects** (what you want)
- **Input Effects** (human actions on system)
- **Feedback Effects** (system actions on humans/other systems)
- **Disturbing Effects** (unwanted external influences)
- **Side Effects** (unwanted outputs from your system)

### 🏠 EVERYDAY ANALOGY

**A Car is Not Just a Car:**

Your car (technical system) exists in context:
- **Human Input:** You turn the steering wheel, press accelerator
- **Intended Effect:** Car moves forward, turns
- **Feedback to Human:** Dashboard shows speed, fuel level
- **Disturbing Effect:** Road ice makes braking difficult (unwanted external)
- **Side Effect:** Exhaust emissions affect pedestrians (unwanted output)

A designer who ignores these interrelationships creates a car that works perfectly in isolation but fails in the real world.

### 🎯 DEFENSE EXAMPLE: Training Grenade

| Effect Type | Example for Training Grenade |
|-------------|------------------------------|
| **Intended Effect** | Simulate explosion for training |
| **Input Effect** | Soldier pulls pin, throws grenade |
| **Feedback Effect** | Loud bang signals "explosion" to trainee |
| **Disturbing Effect** | Rain/moisture may affect fuze ignition |
| **Side Effect** | Smoke may drift toward unintended area, noise affects nearby wildlife |

### ❓ SELF-CHECK QUESTIONS

1. Name all five effect types from memory.
2. Why are "disturbing effects" and "side effects" different? Give example.
3. How does considering system interrelationship help avoid design failures?

### ❌ COMMON MISCONCEPTION

**Wrong:** "Side effects are disturbances from outside"
**Right:** Side effects come FROM your system outward. Disturbances come TO your system from outside.

## 3.2 Chunk 1: Working → Construction Transition (30 min)

**Difficulty:** ⭐ (Introductory)
**Prerequisites:** 2.1.4 Working Interrelationship

### Core Concepts (5 items)
1. Working Structure = HOW function is achieved (principles)
2. Construction Structure = WHAT physically exists (artifacts)
3. Concretisation = Progressive narrowing from abstract → concrete
4. The gap between "principle" and "part"
5. Why transition requires additional considerations

### Explanation

The Working Structure tells us the physical effects and principles that achieve our functions. But a physical effect is not a part you can buy or manufacture. The transition to Construction Structure bridges this gap.

**Example - Towed Target:**
- Working Principle: "Reflect radar signals using conductive surface"
- Construction Question: WHAT conductive surface? What material? What shape? How manufactured? How attached?

The Construction Structure answers these questions with concrete elements:
- Component: Aluminum mesh screen (2m × 1m, 3mm wire, 25mm spacing)
- Assembly: Screen mounted on fiberglass frame
- Interconnection: Riveted joints with rubber isolation mounts

1. System boundary vs. environment

3. **Intended Effect** = Desired output

5. **Feedback Effect** = System action on human/other system
6. **Disturbing Effect** = Unwanted external influence
7. **Side Effect** = Unwanted system output

### The Five Effects Framework

```
                    ┌─────────────────┐
    DISTURBING      │                 │    SIDE
    EFFECTS ───────►│   TECHNICAL     │───────► EFFECTS
   (from outside)   │    SYSTEM       │  (to outside)
                    │                 │
                    └────────┬────────┘
                             │
           ┌─────────────────┼─────────────────┐
           │                 │                 │
           ▼                 │                 ▼
    ┌────────────┐          │          ┌────────────┐
    │   INPUT    │          │          │  FEEDBACK  │
    │  EFFECTS   │          │          │  EFFECTS   │
    │(human→sys) │          │          │(sys→human) │
    └────────────┘          │          └────────────┘
           │                │                 │
           └─────────►┌─────┴─────┐◄──────────┘
                      │   HUMAN    │
                      │ OPERATOR   │
                      └───────────┘
                             │
                             ▼
                      INTENDED EFFECT
                    (desired outcome)
```

| Effect Type | Target USV Example | Design Implication |

### Scoring Rubric

**9-10:** Exceeds expectations. Complete, detailed, production-ready.
**7-8:** Meets expectations. Minor gaps that don't block progress.
**5-6:** Needs improvement. Significant gaps requiring rework.
**3-4:** Inadequate. Major elements missing or incorrect.
**0-2:** Not acceptable. Must restart.

### Common Issues in Defense Projects

| Issue | Impact | Remediation |
|-------|--------|-------------|
| ❌ Unspecified interconnections | Assembly failures, rework | Specify ALL connections with torque, material, tolerance |
| ⚠️ Missing transport consideration | Logistics problems | Add transport constraints to requirements |
| ⚠️ Custom parts without production method | Cost overruns, delays | Identify supplier OR manufacturing process |
| ℹ️ Over-specification | Unnecessary cost | Apply functional tolerancing |

# PART 7: SKILL 6 - CONCEPT EVALUATION ASSISTANT

## 7.1 Evaluating Construction Structure Quality

When comparing alternative construction structures (e.g., two ways to build the same working structure), use these VDI 2225-based criteria:

| Criterion | Weight | Description |
|-----------|--------|-------------|
| **Manufacturability** | 20% | Can be produced with available capabilities |
| **Assembly ease** | 15% | Can be assembled efficiently |
| **Transport feasibility** | 10% | Meets logistical constraints |
| **Component availability** | 15% | Parts can be sourced (especially under export controls) |
| **Maintainability** | 15% | Can be serviced in field |
| **Cost efficiency** | 15% | Reasonable production cost |
| **Reliability** | 10% | Construction enables reliable operation |

### Application Example: Target USV Hull Construction

| Alternative | Manufacturability | Assembly | Transport | Availability | Maintainability | Cost | Reliability | **Total** |
|-------------|------------------|----------|-----------|--------------|-----------------|------|-------------|-----------|

| B: Aluminum | 7 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 8 | 6 | 8 | **7.05** |
| C: Steel | 5 | 5 | 5 | 9 | 6 | 5 | 9 | **6.20** |

**Recommendation:** Fiberglass construction (Alternative A) preferred for Target USV hull due to balance of manufacturability, cost, and local capability.

**Mnemonic:** **"IIFDS" → "II Fải Dành Sức"** (Both sides must invest effort)

### 📖 Component Breakdown
- **I**ntended = Hiệu ứng mong muốn (what you want)
- **I**nput = Tác động đầu vào (human → system)
- **F**eedback = Phản hồi (system → human)
- **D**isturbing = Nhiễu loạn (unwanted from outside)
- **S**ide = Phụ (unwanted from system)

### 💡 Memory Reinforcement
**Story:** "II" represents two parties (human and system) working together. They must "Fải" (must) "Dành" (allocate) "Sức" (effort) - meaning both sides contribute. But watch out for "D" (disturbances from outside trying to disrupt) and "S" (side effects leaking out).

Visualize: Two people (II) shaking hands (cooperation), but rain is falling on them (D=Disturbing) and their movement knocks over a vase (S=Side effect).

### ✅ Quick Recall Test
1. What's the difference between Disturbing and Side effects?
2. Name all 5 effect types.
3. Give an example of Feedback effect for the Radar-IR Target system.
