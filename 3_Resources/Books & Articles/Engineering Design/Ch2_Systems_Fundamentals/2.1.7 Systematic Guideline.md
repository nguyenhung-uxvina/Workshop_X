# PART 2: SKILL 1 - FEYNMAN TECHNIQUE ANALYSIS

## 2.1 The Three General Objectives

### 💡 60-SECOND EXPLANATION

Every technical design must satisfy **THREE GENERAL OBJECTIVES** - like a three-legged stool, if any leg is missing, the design fails:

1. **Technical Function** - The system must actually DO what it's supposed to do
2. **Economic Feasibility** - The system must be affordable to develop, produce, and operate
3. **Safety for Humans and Environment** - The system must not harm people or nature

These are not optional extras. A weapon system that works perfectly but costs 10× budget fails. A cheap system that injures users fails. A technically brilliant, affordable system that pollutes the environment fails.

### 🏠 EVERYDAY ANALOGY

**Building a House:**
- **Technical Function**: The house must keep you warm, dry, and secure
- **Economic Feasibility**: The house must be within your budget
- **Safety**: The house must not collapse, catch fire easily, or emit toxic gases

A house that's warm but unaffordable = failure.
A house that's cheap but unsafe = failure.
A house that's safe and affordable but doesn't keep you warm = failure.

### 🎯 DEFENSE EXAMPLE: Target USV

| Objective | Target USV Application | Failure Mode if Ignored |
|-----------|------------------------|------------------------|
| **Technical Function** | Present realistic target signature, follow commanded paths | If doesn't simulate enemy vessel, training is useless |
| **Economic Feasibility** | Development ≤ $500K, unit cost ≤ $100K, operation ≤ $200/hour | If too expensive, program cancelled |
| **Safety** | No injury to crews, minimal environmental impact | If hazardous, cannot be deployed |

### ❓ SELF-CHECK QUESTIONS

1. Can you name all three general objectives without looking?
2. Why is "technical function alone" not sufficient for design success?
3. Give an example where economic feasibility conflicts with safety - how should this be resolved?

### ❌ COMMON MISCONCEPTION

**Wrong:** "Safety is just another constraint we can trade off against cost"
**Right:** Safety is a GENERAL OBJECTIVE with ethical foundations - it cannot be "optimized away" for cost savings. Cost and schedule requirements must NOT affect safety.

## 2.2 The Ten Constraint Categories

### 💡 60-SECOND EXPLANATION

Beyond the three objectives, every design must address **TEN CATEGORIES OF CONSTRAINTS**. These are like a checklist of "real world" factors that can make or break your design:

```
S-E-P-Q-A-T-O-M-C-R
(Safety-Ergonomics-Production-Quality-Assembly-Transport-Operation-Maintenance-Costs-Recycling)
```

Some constraints are **TASK-SPECIFIC** (unique to your project), others are **GENERAL** (apply to all designs in your industry). Even if not explicitly stated, general constraints must still be satisfied.

### 🏠 EVERYDAY ANALOGY

**Baking a Cake for a Party:**
- **Safety**: Can't use ingredients someone is allergic to
- **Ergonomics**: Must be easy to cut and serve
- **Production**: Must have the right oven and pans
- **Quality Control**: Must check doneness before serving
- **Assembly**: Must be able to layer and frost
- **Transport**: Must survive the car ride to the party
- **Operation**: Must look good on the table, easy to serve
- **Maintenance**: Can't require refrigeration if no fridge at venue
- **Costs**: Must fit the budget
- **Recycling**: Packaging should be disposable/recyclable

### 🎯 DEFENSE EXAMPLE: Complete Constraint Analysis for Training Grenade

| Constraint | Description | Training Grenade Application |
|------------|-------------|------------------------------|
| **Safety** | Reliability, availability, no harm | Fuze must not accidentally detonate; effects must not injure |
| **Ergonomics** | Human-machine interface, aesthetics | Weight/size matches real grenade; intuitive pin-pull mechanism |
| **Production** | Manufacturing facilities, type | Can be produced with available press/molding equipment |
| **Quality Control** | Checks during/after production | 100% fuze testing; batch sampling for casing strength |
| **Assembly** | During and after component production | Fuze-body assembly must be foolproof |
| **Transport** | Inside and outside factory | Must meet transport classification for pyrotechnics |
| **Operation** | Intended use, handling | Safe storage, easy distribution, clear instructions |
| **Maintenance** | Upkeep, inspection, repair | Shelf-life inspection requirements; no field repair needed |
| **Costs** | Development and unit costs | Development ≤ $50K; unit cost ≤ $15 |
| **Recycling** | Reuse, disposal, final storage | Spent casings collected; unexploded rounds safely disposed |

## 3.2 Chunk 1: Three General Objectives (30 min)

**Difficulty:** ⭐
**Prerequisites:** Basic understanding of design process

### Core Concepts (5 items)
1. Technical Function as primary objective
2. Economic Feasibility as essential requirement
3. Safety for humans and environment as ethical imperative
4. Interdependence of the three objectives
5. None can be sacrificed for another (especially safety)

### Explanation

Pahl & Beitz states clearly: "The fulfilment of the technical function alone does not complete the task of designers; it would simply be an end unto itself."

This means a designer who creates a technically perfect solution that is unaffordable or unsafe has FAILED. The three objectives form a triangle of constraints:

```
              TECHNICAL FUNCTION
                     △
                    /│\
                   / │ \
                  /  │  \
                 /   │   \
                /    │    \
               /     │     \
              /      │      \
             /       │       \
            ▼        ▼        ▼
    ECONOMIC ←───────────────→ SAFETY
    FEASIBILITY               (Human & Environment)
```

**Trade-off Rules:**
- Technical vs. Economic: Can simplify function to reduce cost
- Technical vs. Safety: NEVER compromise safety for function
- Economic vs. Safety: NEVER compromise safety for cost ("Cost limits and delivery dates are ensured by careful planning... not by cutting corners")

### Defense Application: Radar-IR Target Simulation

| Objective | Specific Target | Trade-off Decision |
|-----------|----------------|-------------------|
| Technical | Simulate enemy aircraft signature | Must be realistic enough for sensor testing |
| Economic | Unit cost ≤ $50K | Can use commercial electronics vs. mil-spec |
| Safety | No RF hazard to ground crew | Non-negotiable - must include safety interlocks |

1. Technical function objective (what must it do?)
2. Economic feasibility bounds (what's reasonable cost?)
3. Safety requirements (what could harm humans/environment?)
4. One trade-off you might face between technical and economic

### Task-Specific vs. General Constraints

**Task-Specific Constraints:** Unique to your project
- Example: "Target USV must fit in standard shipping container" (specific to this project)

**General Constraints:** Apply to all similar designs
- Example: "All maritime systems must comply with COLREGS" (applies to all vessels)

**Key Insight:** General constraints are often IMPLICIT - not written in requirements but still mandatory. Experienced designers know them; novices must learn them.

### Defense Application: Xuồng Cứu hộ (Rescue Boat)

| Category | Task-Specific | General (Implicit) |
|----------|---------------|-------------------|

| Ergonomics | Rescue platform height 0.5m | Controls accessible while wearing gloves |
| Production | Hull must be produced locally | Standard marine construction practices |
| Quality | 100% pressure test for hulls | ISO 9001 documentation |
| Assembly | Modular for depot-level maintenance | Standard naval assembly procedures |
| Transport | Fits on 5-ton truck | Road transport regulations |
| Operation | 2-person crew minimum | Standard rescue procedures |
| Maintenance | 3-level military system | Preventive maintenance schedule |
| Costs | Unit cost ≤ $80K | Life-cycle cost analysis |
| Recycling | Aluminum hull recyclable | Hazmat disposal for batteries |

**Difficulty:** ⭐⭐

### The Embodiment Design Checklist (Figure 7.3)

Pahl & Beitz provides Figure 7.3 as a **practical checklist** derived from Section 2.1.7. This checklist is used during Embodiment Design (Phase 3) to systematically verify all constraints.

| Heading | Example Questions to Ask |
|---------|-------------------------|
| **Function** | Is the stipulated function fulfilled? What auxiliary functions are needed? |
| **Working Principle** | Do the chosen working principles produce desired effects? What disturbing factors expected? |
| **Layout** | Do shapes, materials, dimensions provide: strength, stiffness, stability, freedom from resonance, acceptable wear? |
| **Safety** | Have all safety factors been considered for components, function, operation, environment? |
| **Ergonomics** | Human-machine relationships considered? Unnecessary stress avoided? Aesthetics addressed? |
| **Production** | Technological and economic analysis of production processes done? |
| **Quality Control** | Necessary checks specified? Required time defined? |
| **Assembly** | Can internal and external assembly be performed simply and correctly? |
| **Transport** | Internal and external transport conditions and risks examined? |
| **Operation** | All factors influencing operation (noise, vibration, handling) considered? |
| **Maintenance** | Can maintenance, inspection, overhaul be easily performed and checked? |
| **Recycling** | Can product be reused or recycled? |
| **Costs** | Stipulated cost limits observed? Additional operational costs possible? |
| **Schedules** | Can delivery dates be met? Design modifications that might improve delivery? |

**Sample Checklist Review:**

| Heading | Question | Status | Notes |

## 4.1 Review Criteria for Goals and Constraints Application

### Phase-Specific Checklist

| Criterion | Score (0-10) | Evidence Required |
|-----------|--------------|-------------------|
| **Three objectives defined** | | Technical, Economic, Safety targets specified |
| **Objective conflicts identified** | | Trade-off analysis documented |
| **Safety prioritized** | | No safety compromises for cost/schedule |

### Scoring Rubric

**9-10:** All objectives and constraints fully defined with verification plan
**7-8:** Complete coverage with minor gaps in verification
**5-6:** Most constraints addressed but some general constraints missed
**3-4:** Significant gaps in constraint coverage
**0-2:** Goals and constraints not systematically considered

### Common Issues in Defense Projects

| Issue | Impact | Remediation |
|-------|--------|-------------|
| ❌ Safety compromised for cost | Ethical violation, potential harm | Non-negotiable: redesign within cost OR increase budget |
| ❌ General constraints missed | Late discovery causes redesign | Use industry checklist early; consult experienced engineers |
| ⚠️ Implicit requirements unstated | Miscommunication with production/users | Make ALL constraints explicit in requirements list |
| ⚠️ Verification method unclear | Cannot prove compliance | Define verification (test, analysis, inspection) for each |
| ℹ️ Over-constrained | Impossible to satisfy all | Prioritize; negotiate relaxation of wishes (not demands) |

# PART 10: SKILL 9 - SYSTEMS MAPPER

## 10.1 System Dynamics of Goals and Constraints

### The Constraint Trade-off System

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│            CONSTRAINT TRADE-OFF SYSTEM DYNAMICS                 │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

STOCKS:
[Design Freedom]      - Options available to designer
[Budget Remaining]    - Financial resources
[Schedule Margin]     - Time buffer
[Safety Confidence]   - Belief in system safety

FLOWS:
→ Constraint Application (-design freedom, as constraints applied)
→ Budget Consumption (-budget, as design progresses)
→ Schedule Erosion (-margin, as time passes)
→ Testing (+safety confidence, -budget, -schedule)

FEEDBACK LOOPS:

B1: Budget-Design Loop (Balancing)
   [Budget Pressure] → [Cheaper Solutions] →
   [More Constraints Violated] → [Rework Required] →
   [Higher Actual Cost] → [More Budget Pressure]

B2: Schedule-Quality Loop (Balancing)
   [Schedule Pressure] → [Skip Verification] →
   [Undetected Issues] → [Late Discovery] →
   [More Schedule Pressure]

R1: Safety-Cost-Schedule Trap (Reinforcing - DANGEROUS)
   [Schedule/Cost Pressure] → [Safety Compromise] →
   [Accident/Failure] → [Investigation/Rework] →
   [Much Higher Cost/Schedule Impact]

CRITICAL INSIGHT: Loop R1 shows why "Cost and schedule requirements must NOT affect safety" - the reinforcing loop leads to disaster.
```

### Leverage Points for Constraint Management

| Level | Intervention | Application |
|-------|-------------|-------------|
| L12 (Parameters) | Adjust specific cost target | "Increase unit cost budget 10%" |
| L9 (Delays) | Faster constraint verification | "Test earlier, fail faster" |
| L6 (Information) | Real-time constraint status dashboard | "All stakeholders see constraint violations immediately" |
| L5 (Rules) | "Safety constraints never negotiable" policy | "Any safety violation requires VP approval" |
| L3 (Goals) | "Best value" not "lowest cost" | "Optimize for lifecycle, not procurement" |
