## 1. Overview of Architecture Families

Analysis of the 10 LOMAH systems reveals **four distinct architecture families** based on their fundamental detection and processing approaches:

### Family A: Open-Array Acoustic TDOA (7 of 10 systems)

This is the dominant architecture. An open-air bar or frame carries an array of acoustic sensors (typically 4-8 per target) arranged in delta or H-type configurations. Sensors detect the supersonic shockwave (N-wave) generated by a bullet traveling above Mach 1. Time-Difference-of-Arrival (TDOA) between sensor pairs is computed, and hyperbolic intersection algorithms solve for the bullet's X,Y coordinates in the target plane. A temperature sensor compensates for speed-of-sound variations.

**Systems:** Steinert TrueZeroTarget, InVeris LOMAH, Theissen LOMAH (supersonic mode), Oakwood H-Bar, Saab LOMAH, Zen Technologies LOMAH, BEL DHVANI.

**Core limitation:** Requires supersonic ammunition (typically >Mach 1.3 / ~440 m/s at the target). Cannot score pistol-caliber subsonic rounds.

### Family B: Closed-Chamber Acoustic (2 of 10 systems)

An enclosed, soundproofed chamber surrounds the bullet passage zone. Acoustic sensors (typically 4, one per corner) inside the sealed chamber detect the pressure wave caused by any projectile passing through the rubber membrane -- including subsonic rounds. The sealed chamber eliminates wind noise, weather effects, and ambient acoustic interference, enabling extreme accuracy (sub-millimeter in the case of Kongsberg eScore). Temperature shields maintain uniform internal conditions.

**Systems:** Kongsberg eScore (sport/competition), Theissen Box Target (subsonic mode).

**Core trade-off:** Achieves highest accuracy and all-velocity capability, but requires a fixed, enclosed frame and periodic rubber membrane replacement. Not suited for open tactical deployment.

### Family C: Radar-Based Detection (1 of 10 systems)

Polytronic's subsonic LOMAH uses radar (likely CW Doppler or FMCW -- specific modulation is proprietary) to detect and localize subsonic projectiles that produce no supersonic shockwave. The radar emits microwave energy and detects the Doppler-shifted return from the bullet in flight as it passes through the detection window near the target plane. Multiple radar transceivers at known positions use Doppler history or time-of-flight differences to triangulate the bullet's X,Y position.

**Systems:** Polytronic Subsonic LOMAH (world's first; used in combination with acoustic H-Bar/T-Bar for supersonic).

**Core trade-off:** Only system capable of scoring truly subsonic ammunition in an open-air configuration. However, radar introduces RF emissions, higher power consumption, and more complex signal processing.

### Family D: Hybrid Acoustic + Radar (1 of 10 systems, combining Families A and C)

Polytronic's complete LOMAH offering combines an acoustic H-Bar or T-Bar for supersonic ammunition with the radar-based subsonic LOMAH unit. This allows a single target lane to score all ammunition types -- from 5.56 mm supersonic rifle to 9 mm subsonic pistol -- supporting weapon-transition drills in the same session without reconfiguration.

**Systems:** Polytronic full-spectrum LOMAH deployment.

| Dimension | Details |
|---|---|

Published research on small-projectile radar tracking describes using ~1 Watt CW transceivers spaced ~1 m apart to estimate trajectory angle to +/-1 degree for bullets passing within 10 m. ## 13. Key Architectural Insights for BB-01 Design

The following architectural insights are directly relevant to the design of the BB-01 LOMAH system:

### Insight 1: The TDOA Algorithm Is Universal -- Differentiation Is in the Implementation

All 10 systems use acoustic TDOA as their primary or sole detection algorithm (9 of 10 for supersonic; Polytronic adds radar for subsonic). The mathematical foundation -- hyperbolic intersection from time-difference measurements -- is well-established and covered by foundational patents (US 5,920,522; US 6,669,477) that are now expired or nearing expiration. **BB-01 does not need to invent a new detection algorithm.** The differentiation between systems lies in: (a) sensor quality and noise rejection, (b) timing resolution of the ADC/timer capture, (c) temperature compensation accuracy, and (d) the robustness of the N-wave detection (distinguishing bullets from environmental noise). These are engineering-quality dimensions, not fundamental algorithmic challenges.

### Insight 2: Sensor Count and Geometry Drive Accuracy and Capability

The data shows a clear correlation:
- **3-4 sensors** (Falcon Kestrel, Kongsberg eScore): sufficient for basic 2D TDOA but requires controlled conditions or sealed chamber for good accuracy.
- **6 sensors** (Oakwood H-Bar): enables +/- 5 mm accuracy with temperature compensation and up to 15-degree off-angle capability.
- **8 sensors / dual delta** (InVeris, Polytronic, TTS, Saab): enables +/- 3 mm accuracy, redundancy, and significant off-angle capability.
- **BB-01 should target 6-8 sensors** in a dual-delta or H-type configuration to achieve +/- 3-5 mm accuracy with off-angle tolerance. More sensors than 8 provide diminishing returns.

### Insight 3: Temperature Compensation Is Non-Negotiable for Outdoor Accuracy

Every system that publishes accuracy specifications explicitly mentions temperature compensation. The speed of sound varies by ~0.6 m/s per degree Celsius. Over a 100 mm sensor spacing, a 10 degC uncompensated error introduces ~0.2 mm position error. For +/- 3 mm accuracy, temperature compensation is essential. Steinert and Oakwood use dedicated temperature sensors. Kongsberg uses temperature shields and even temperature management in the sealed chamber. **BB-01 must include a precision temperature sensor (e.g., digital thermometer with +/- 0.5 degC accuracy) integrated with the sensor bar.**

### Insight 4: On-Target Embedded Processing Is the Modern Trend for Portable Systems

The portable systems (Steinert, Oakwood, Falcon) all perform TDOA computation on-target and transmit results wirelessly. The fixed-installation systems (Polytronic, TTS, InVeris fixed) send raw/preprocessed data to centralized servers. **For BB-01 as a portable system, on-target embedded processing using an MCU/FPGA with Wi-Fi output is the correct architecture.** This eliminates the need for a wired control link and enables smartphone/tablet display.

### Insight 5: LiFePO4 Is the Preferred Battery Chemistry for Field LOMAH

Steinert's choice of LiFePO4 (3.2V, 12Ah, 16 hours runtime) is notable. LiFePO4 offers superior thermal stability (-30 to +70 degC operating range), longer cycle life (2000+ cycles), inherent safety (no thermal runaway), and flat discharge curve -- all critical for field military equipment. **BB-01 should use LiFePO4 cells.** The 12 Ah capacity and 16-hour runtime of the Steinert system sets a good benchmark.

### Insight 6: The Sealed-Chamber Architecture Provides a Pathway to Subsonic Detection Without Radar

Kongsberg eScore and TTS Box Target demonstrate that sealing the detection zone in a soundproof chamber enables subsonic bullet detection using purely acoustic methods -- no radar needed. The sealed chamber detects the aerodynamic pressure disturbance of any projectile, not just the supersonic N-wave. **If BB-01 needs subsonic capability in the future, a sealed-chamber variant is architecturally simpler and cheaper than adding radar.** However, this requires a fixed frame with replaceable rubber membrane, which conflicts with portability.

### Insight 7: The BEL DHVANI "Life-Sciences Inspired Algorithm" Suggests Cost-Optimized DSP Is Achievable

DHVANI's 50-60% cost reduction while maintaining "comparable" accuracy suggests that biomimetic or advanced signal processing algorithms can compensate for cheaper sensor hardware. **BB-01 should invest in robust N-wave detection and noise rejection algorithms (potentially biomimetic or ML-based) rather than relying solely on high-end sensor hardware.** This is a software-driven cost optimization strategy. The N-wave identification approach (detecting the characteristic pressure signature before performing TDOA) is a valuable false-positive rejection technique that should be implemented in BB-01.
