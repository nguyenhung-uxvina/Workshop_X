## Key Observations and Trends

1. **Acoustic TDOA dominance with emerging alternatives:** All 10 systems use acoustic time-difference-of-arrival as their primary detection method for supersonic projectiles. However, Polytronic's radar-based subsonic LOMAH represents a significant technological breakthrough -- it is the only system that can score pistol-caliber (9 mm) subsonic ammunition without requiring bullet contact with the target. Kongsberg's closed-chamber eScore also handles subsonic rounds by containing the acoustic signature. These hybrid/alternative approaches are likely to expand as forces demand consolidated scoring across all weapon types.

2. **Accuracy convergence at +/- 3 to 5 mm:** Military-grade systems have converged on a +/- 3 to 5 mm accuracy standard for acoustic LOMAH. This is more than adequate for marksmanship training at ranges from 100 m to 1,000 m, where even expert-level shot groups are measured in centimeters. Kongsberg's eScore closed-chamber system achieves sub-millimeter accuracy suitable for Olympic competition, but this level of precision requires a fundamentally different (enclosed) target architecture.

3. **Market stratification by price and audience:** The market now spans three clear tiers: (a) consumer/personal ($2,000 - $5,500: Falcon Kestrel, Oakwood H-Bar), (b) mid-range institutional ($3,500 - $10,000: Steinert TrueZeroTarget, BEL DHVANI), and (c) military-institutional (typically $10,000+ per system but sold as range-level contracts: InVeris, Polytronic, TTS, Kongsberg, Saab). The consumer tier is new as of 2024-2025 and is being driven by the long-range precision shooting community.

4. **Portability vs. integration trade-off:** A clear design tension exists between standalone portable systems (Steinert, Oakwood, Falcon) and fully integrated range ecosystems (Polytronic, InVeris, Saab, Kongsberg). Portable systems excel in flexibility (battery-powered, Wi-Fi, rapid deployment) but lack features like centralized range management, reactive target behavior, and multi-lane orchestration. The InVeris Portable LOMAH and Zen Technologies systems attempt to bridge this gap with wireless communication to a Master Control Station.

5. **Indian indigenous capability is rapidly maturing:** Both Zen Technologies (private sector) and BEL/CSIR-NAL (government sector) offer competitive LOMAH systems at significantly lower price points (50-60% cost reduction claimed by BEL). India's "Make in India" defense policy is driving domestic production, and these systems are beginning to appear in export markets across South and Southeast Asia and Africa. While detailed published specifications lag behind Western competitors, operational deployment with the Indian Army validates their capability.

6. **Environmental hardening varies significantly:** IP ratings range from IP66 (Steinert) to IP67 (TTS) among those that publish ratings, while several major manufacturers (InVeris, Polytronic, Saab) do not publicly disclose environmental protection specifications. Operating temperature ranges span from -30 degC (Steinert, Kongsberg) to +70 degC (Steinert), with most systems designed for at least -25 to +50 degC. For arctic or extreme-desert operations, buyers should specifically verify environmental ratings against their operational envelope -- this is an area where datasheet gaps can hide meaningful capability differences.
